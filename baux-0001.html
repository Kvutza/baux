Baux

baux is a black box optimization library for expensive or noisy functions \(f(x)\) evaluated over small sample budgets, from low-dimensional design parameters up to \(10^5\)-weight control policies. At each query \(x\), Epistemic Nearest Neighbors (Bafna, Jadhav, & Sweet [BJS25]; yubo-research/enn) predicts value and uncertainty from the \(K\) closest evaluated points weighted by inverse variance, avoiding global \(N \times N\) covariance matrix inversion as observations accumulate.

For high-dimensional or multimodal objectives, baux runs parallel local trust regions (Eriksson et al. [EPG+19]) that perturb sparse coordinate subsets around regional incumbents. Observation arrays, regional side lengths and counters, pending asynchronous tickets, and a splittable 128-bit PRNG key live in an immutable state object advanced by pure functions (API Reference).

A basic loop pairs an ENN surrogate (baux.enn) with upper-confidence acquisition (baux.ucb) and updates state via baux.init, baux.ask, and baux.tell:

import numpy as np
import baux


def objective(x):
    return -np.sum((x - 0.4) ** 2, axis=1)


bounds = np.tile([-2.0, 2.0], (6, 1))
pipe = baux.enn(k=10) | baux.ucb(beta=2.0)

state = baux.init(bounds, pipe, key=baux.key(0))
for _ in range(15):
    state, batch = baux.ask(state, num_arms=4)
    state, _ = baux.tell(state, batch, objective(batch.points))

assert state.best_x.shape == (6,) and state.best_y > -0.25

1. Neighborhood Surrogates

1.1. Inverse-Variance Prediction from \(K\) Nearest Neighbors

Gaussian process surrogates factor an \(N \times N\) kernel covariance matrix in \(\mathcal {O}(N^3)\) time. Given \(N\) noisy observations \(\mathcal {D} = \{(x_m, y_m, s_m)\}_{m=1}^N \subset \mathcal {X} \times \mathbb {R} \times \mathbb {R}_+\) modeled as \(y_m = f(x_m) + s_m \varepsilon _m\) with \(\varepsilon _m \stackrel {\text {i.i.d.}}{\sim } \mathcal {N}(0, 1)\), Epistemic Nearest Neighbors (Bafna et al. [BJS25]) forms local predictions at \(x \in \mathcal {X}\) from a pairwise dissimilarity \(\delta : \mathcal {X} \times \mathcal {X} \to \mathbb {R}_+\), empirical target variance \(\hat {\sigma }_y^2 > 0\), and scale parameters \(\theta = (s_0^2, c_e) \in \mathbb {R}_+^2\). Each observation \(m\) contributes an observation-noise variance and a distance-proportional epistemic variance:

\[ \mu (x \mid x_m) = y_m, \qquad \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_m) = \hat {\sigma }_y^2 s_0^2 + s_m^2, \qquad \sigma _e^2(x \mid x_m) = \hat {\sigma }_y^2 c_e\, \delta (x, x_m). \]

Let \(N_K(x) = \{\pi _1(x), \dots , \pi _K(x)\}\) list the \(K\) nearest indices ordered by nondecreasing \(\delta (x, x_{\pi _i(x)})\) (breaking ties by datum index), with inverse-variance weights \(w_i(x) = \bigl (\sigma _a^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)}) + \sigma _e^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)})\bigr )^{-1}\). An \(\mathcal {O}(N D + N \log K)\) scan per query yields the local mean, aleatoric variance, and epistemic variance:

\[ \mu (x) = \frac {\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)\, y_{\pi _i(x)}}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \qquad \sigma _a^2(x) = \frac {\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)\, \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)})}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \qquad \sigma _e^2(x) = \frac {1}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \]

with total predictive variance \(\sigma ^2(x) = \sigma _e^2(x) + \sigma _a^2(x)\). The scale parameters \(\theta = (s_0^2, c_e)\) maximize a leave-one-out Gaussian log-likelihood over a subsample \(S_P \subset \{1, \dots , N\}\) of size \(P \le N\) [BJS25]:

\[ \widehat {\log \mathcal {L}}(\theta ) = -\frac {1}{2P} \sum _{n \in S_P} \left [\log \bigl (2\pi \sigma _{-n}^2(x_n; \theta )\bigr ) + \frac {\bigl (y_n - \mu _{-n}(x_n; \theta )\bigr )^2}{\sigma _{-n}^2(x_n; \theta )}\right ], \]

where \(\mu _{-n}\) and \(\sigma _{-n}^2\) omit observation \(n\) from its \(K\)-neighbor set.

1.2. Correlated Posterior Draws

Thompson sampling over \(Q\) candidate points requires function draws that covary across nearby queries without factoring a \(Q \times Q\) joint covariance matrix. For draw \(s\) and output channel \(j\), attaching one independent normal variable \(z_{s,m,j} \stackrel {\text {i.i.d.}}{\sim } \mathcal {N}(0, 1)\) to each stored observation \(m \in \{1, \dots , N\}\) couples queries through their shared neighbors.

Let \(\bar {w}_{i,j}(x) = w_{i,j}(x) / \sum _{r=1}^K w_{r,j}(x)\) denote normalized weights on \(N_K(x)\), extended to \(m \in \{1, \dots , N\}\) by \(\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x) = \bar {w}_{i,j}(x)\) when \(m = \pi _i(x)\) and \(0\) otherwise, and set \(\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2 = \bigl (\sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}^2(x)\bigr )^{1/2}\). Rescaling the weighted sum of neighbor draws preserves the marginal epistemic variance \(\sigma _{e,j}^2(x)\) at every \(x\):

\[ \tilde {f}_{s,j}(x) = \mu _j(x) + \frac {\sigma _{e,j}(x)}{\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2} \sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}(x)\, z_{s,\pi _i(x),j} \sim \mathcal {N}\bigl (\mu _j(x),\, \sigma _{e,j}^2(x)\bigr ). \]

Sample paths vary smoothly inside each order-\(K\) Voronoi cell where \(N_K(x)\) is fixed, and any two queries with overlapping neighborhoods \(S_K(x, x') = N_K(x) \cap N_K(x') \ne \emptyset \) share positive covariance:

\[ \operatorname {Cov}\bigl (\tilde {f}_{s,j}(x),\, \tilde {f}_{s,j}(x')\bigr ) = \frac {\sigma _{e,j}(x)\,\sigma _{e,j}(x')}{\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2\,\Vert \bar {w}_j(x')\Vert _2} \sum _{m \in S_K(x, x')} \tilde {w}_{m,j}(x)\,\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x') > 0. \]

1.3. Local-Scale Distances under Uneven Sampling

Iterative search concentrates evaluations tightly inside active trust-region basins while leaving the rest of the domain sparsely sampled. Under plain Euclidean distance \(d(x, x') = \Vert x - x'\Vert _2\), a dense cluster can absorb coarse outer points into its \(K\)-neighbor set or pull in distant exploratory queries. Letting \(\sigma (x) > 0\) denote the distance from \(x\) to its \(k_0\)-th nearest observation in \(\mathcal {D}\), three rescaled dissimilarities adjust neighbor selection to local point spacing:

  • Conformal scaling (Zelnik-Manor & Perona [ZP04]) divides squared Euclidean distance by the geometric mean of endpoint spacings: \[ \delta _c(x, x_m) = \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)}. \]
  • Poincaré half-space distance (Cannon et al. [CFK+97], Nickel & Kiela [NK17], Kisfaludi-Bak & van Wordragen [KvW24]) associates each point with \((x, \sigma (x)) \in \mathcal {X} \times \mathbb {R}_+\) under \(ds^2 = (\Vert dx\Vert _2^2 + d\sigma ^2)/\sigma ^2\). Evaluating \(\cosh d_{\mathbb {H}} - 1\) adds a scale-difference penalty to conformal distance: \[ \delta _h(x, x_m) = \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{2\,\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)} + \frac {\bigl (\sigma (x) - \sigma (x_m)\bigr )^2}{2\,\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)}. \] When \(\sigma (x) \ll \sigma (x_m)\), the second term grows as \(\mathcal {O}(\sigma (x_m)/\sigma (x))\), keeping fine-scale basin neighborhoods separate from coarse outer points and preventing sparse queries from collapsing onto dense clusters.
  • Hybrid transport dissimilarity, motivated by Gaussian Bures–Wasserstein coupling (Villani [Vil09], Peyré & Cuturi [PC19]), combines root-geometric scaling with an additive spacing term: \[ \delta _w(x, x_m) = \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{\sqrt {\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)}} + \bigl (\sigma (x) - \sigma (x_m)\bigr )^2. \]

The standalone functional interface (baux.enn_init, baux.with_geometry, baux.enn_fit, baux.enn_posterior, and baux.enn_sample) fits and queries these geometries directly. On a 6D design combining a dense low-noise basin with a sparse high-noise outer shell, hyperbolic distance selects in-basin neighbors, separates epistemic (.se_epi) from aleatoric (.se_ale) standard error, and produces correlated draws across adjacent basin queries:

import numpy as np
import baux

rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
basin_x = 0.6 + 0.04 * rng.normal(size=(24, 6))
shell_x = rng.uniform(-1.5, 1.5, size=(24, 6))
train_x = np.vstack([basin_x, shell_x])

signal = np.exp(-1.5 * np.sum((train_x - 0.6) ** 2, axis=1, keepdims=True))
train_yvar = np.vstack([np.full((24, 1), 5e-4), np.full((24, 1), 5e-2)])
train_y = signal + np.sqrt(train_yvar) * rng.normal(size=(48, 1))

k_fit, k_draw = baux.split(baux.key(11), 2)
enn_state = baux.with_geometry(
    baux.enn_init(train_x, train_y, train_yvar, scale_x=True),
    "hyperbolic",
    local_k=4,
)
params = baux.enn_fit(enn_state, k=8, infer_aleatoric=False, key=k_fit)

queries = np.vstack([
    np.full((1, 6), 0.600),
    np.full((1, 6), 0.601),
    np.full((1, 6), 2.400),
])
post = baux.enn_posterior(
    enn_state, queries, params, flags=baux.enn.PosteriorFlags(observation_noise=True)
)
draws, idx = baux.enn_sample(enn_state, queries, params, key=k_draw, num_draws=64)

corr_basin = float(np.corrcoef(draws[0, 0], draws[1, 0])[0, 1])
assert np.all(idx[0] < 24)
assert post.se_epi[0, 0] < post.se_epi[2, 0]
assert np.allclose(post.se**2, post.se_epi**2 + post.se_ale**2)
assert corr_basin > 0.8

2. Local Trust Regions & Functional State

2.1. Multi-Region Search & Asynchronous Evaluation

Over high-dimensional domains, global uncertainty grows toward box boundaries, pulling unconstrained acquisition away from promising basins. baux.regions maintains \(R\) local axis-aligned trust regions \(\{T_r(x_{0,r}, \ell _r, g_r)\}_{r=1}^R\) centered at regional incumbents \(x_{0,r}\) with side lengths \(\ell _r\) and restart generation \(g_r\) (Eriksson et al. [EPG+19], Daulton et al. [DEB+22]). Consecutive improvements double \(\ell _r\), consecutive stalls halve \(\ell _r\) down to a restart threshold, and candidate pools perturb sparse coordinate subsets around \(x_{0,r}\) when \(D\) is large. On multi-objective problems (\(M \ge 2\)), each region scalarizes normalized outputs with simplex weights [DEB+22].

Cross-region incumbent updates follow one of two rules: sharing="origin_only" updates \(x_{0,r}\) only from evaluations proposed by region \(r\), whereas sharing="ellipsoid_ball" adopts any higher-scoring global observation inside the Euclidean ball circumscribing \(T_r\)'s box in solver coordinates. When evaluations run asynchronously, each proposed arm carries an AskId ticket \((r, g, a)\) recording its originating region \(r\), dispatch generation \(g = g_r\), and slot \(a\). Out-of-order completions via baux.complete always append observations to \(\mathcal {D}\), while side-length updates on \(T_r\) apply only when \(g = g_r\) so delayed evaluations from before a restart do not alter the re-centered region. baux.cancel releases abandoned slots without modifying \(\mathcal {D}\).

2.2. Deterministic Rollouts & Checkpointing

Each OptimizerState pairs observation arrays and regional trust-region state with a splittable 128-bit PRNG key (Claessen & Pałka [CP13], Steele, Lea, & Flood [SLF14]). Sequential loops via baux.scan and pre-split parallel branches \(k \mapsto (k_1, \dots , k_B)\) via baux.pmap produce deterministic trajectories independent of worker thread scheduling.

baux.freeze and baux.thaw serialize the full search state—observations, surrogate fit state, trust-region side lengths and success/failure counters, pending asynchronous tickets, and PRNG state—into an authenticated binary snapshot summarized by baux.digest. Restoring against a domain-compatible template state \(s_0\) satisfies \(\mathrm {thaw}(s_0, \mathrm {freeze}(s)) = s\), so resumed runs propose identical candidate batches and parallel rollouts:

import numpy as np
import baux

bounds = np.tile([-3.0, 3.0], (8, 1))
weights = np.geomspace(1.0, 16.0, 8)


def anisotropic_loss(x):
    return -np.sum(weights * (x - 0.35) ** 2 + 0.1 * np.cos(3.0 * x), axis=1)


pipe = (
    baux.lhd(8)
    | baux.enn(k=6)
    | baux.thompson()
    | baux.regions(count=4, sharing="ellipsoid_ball")
)
s0 = baux.init(bounds, pipe, key=baux.key(101))
s_warm, b_init = baux.ask(s0, num_arms=8)
s_warm = baux.complete(s_warm, b_init.ids, anisotropic_loss(b_init.points))

s_live, b1 = baux.ask(s_warm, num_arms=2)
s_live, b2 = baux.ask(s_live, num_arms=2)
s_live = baux.complete(s_live, [b2.ids[0]], anisotropic_loss(b2.points[:1]))
s_live = baux.cancel(s_live, [b2.ids[1]])
s_live = baux.complete(s_live, b1.ids, anisotropic_loss(b1.points))

blob = baux.freeze(s_live)
restored_a = baux.thaw(s0, blob)
restored_b = baux.thaw(s0, baux.freeze(restored_a))
assert baux.freeze(restored_a) == blob
assert baux.digest(s_live) == baux.digest(restored_a) == baux.digest(restored_b)

rollouts_live = baux.pmap(
    s_live,
    baux.key(77),
    branches=4,
    branch_fn=lambda i, st, k: baux.digest(
        baux.scan(st, k, steps=4, eval_fn=anisotropic_loss, num_arms=2)[0]
    ),
)
rollouts_a = baux.pmap(
    restored_a,
    baux.key(77),
    branches=4,
    branch_fn=lambda i, st, k: baux.digest(
        baux.scan(st, k, steps=4, eval_fn=anisotropic_loss, num_arms=2)[0]
    ),
)
rollouts_b = baux.pmap(
    restored_b,
    baux.key(77),
    branches=4,
    branch_fn=lambda i, st, k: baux.digest(
        baux.scan(st, k, steps=4, eval_fn=anisotropic_loss, num_arms=2)[0]
    ),
)
assert rollouts_live == rollouts_a == rollouts_b and len(set(rollouts_live)) == 4

3. Structured Inputs & Multi-Output Shrinkage

3.1. Parameter Coordinate Maps

Domains passed to baux.init can be nested Python dicts, tuples, or dataclasses (PyTrees) combining positive scale variables, periodic angles, mixture weights, and discrete choices \(\mathcal {M} = \prod _{\ell =1}^L \mathcal {M}_\ell \). Each leaf specification maps parameter values \(x \in \mathcal {M}_\ell \) into flat solver coordinates \(\psi _\ell : \mathcal {M}_\ell \to \mathcal {U}_\ell \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{d_\ell }\) where neighbor distances and trust-region boxes are computed, and decodes proposals back via \(\rho _\ell : \mathcal {U}_\ell \to \mathcal {M}_\ell \) with \(\rho _\ell (\psi _\ell (x)) = x\):

  • Log-scale intervals (baux.Log): On \([a, b] \subset \mathbb {R}_+\), \[ \psi (x) = \frac {\log x - \log a}{\log b - \log a} \in [0, 1], \qquad \rho (u) = a\left (\frac {b}{a}\right )^{\Pi _{[0,1]}(u)}, \] so uniform steps in \(u\) correspond to constant multiplicative ratios in \(x\).
  • Periodic intervals (baux.Periodic): On \(\lbrack a, b\rparen \) of period \(P = b - a\) with phase \(\phi (x) = \tfrac {2\pi (x - a)}{P}\), circle embedding and \(\operatorname {atan2}\) decoding, \[ \psi (x) = \left (\frac {1}{2} + \frac {\cos \phi (x)}{2\pi },\; \frac {1}{2} + \frac {\sin \phi (x)}{2\pi }\right ), \qquad d_{\mathcal {U}}\bigl (\psi (x), \psi (x')\bigr ) = \frac {1}{\pi }\left |\sin \left (\frac {\phi (x) - \phi (x')}{2}\right )\right |, \] avoid artificial jumps across the wrap boundary \(a \equiv b\).
  • Probability simplices (baux.Simplex): On \(\Delta ^{d-1}\), the square-root map onto the positive unit sphere and radial normalization, \[ \psi (p) = \bigl (\sqrt {p_1}, \dots , \sqrt {p_d}\bigr ) \in S_+^{d-1}, \qquad \rho (u) = \frac {\max (u, 0)^{\odot 2}}{\Vert \max (u, 0)\Vert _2^2}, \] make squared Euclidean distance in \(\mathcal {U}\) equal to squared Hellinger distance \(\sum _{j=1}^d \bigl (\sqrt {p_j} - \sqrt {q_j}\bigr )^2\).
  • Discrete sets (baux.categorical): Finite choices \(\{1, \dots , C\}\) map to equal-norm orthogonal or Rademacher codewords \(\psi (c) \in \mathbb {R}^{d_c}\), reducing nearest-codeword decoding to \(\rho (u) = \arg \max _c \langle u, \psi (c)\rangle \).

3.2. Linear Subspace Shrinkage

When each evaluation returns \(M\) noisy outputs \(y = \theta + \eta \in \mathbb {R}^M\) concentrated near a low-rank subspace, baux.Stiefel orthonormalizes a basis matrix \(L \in \mathbb {R}^{M \times r}\) (\(1 \le r < M\)) into \(V = L(L^\top L)^{-1/2}\) with orthogonal complement \(V_\perp \). Writing \(z_\parallel = V^\top y\) and \(z_\perp = V_\perp ^\top y\), a linear shrinkage estimator leaves components in \(\operatorname {col}(V)\) unchanged and damps the orthogonal residual by \(\alpha \in [0, 1]\) (Stein [Ste56], James & Stein [JS61]):

\[ \hat {\theta }_\alpha = V V^\top y + \alpha (I_M - V V^\top ) y = V z_\parallel + \alpha V_\perp z_\perp . \]

With zero-mean noise variances \(v_\parallel = \mathbb {E}\Vert V^\top \eta \Vert _2^2\) and \(v_\perp = \mathbb {E}\Vert V_\perp ^\top \eta \Vert _2^2 > 0\) and off-subspace signal energy \(b_\perp ^2 = \Vert V_\perp ^\top \theta \Vert _2^2\), the mean squared error \(\mathcal {R}(\alpha ) = \mathbb {E}\Vert \hat {\theta }_\alpha - \theta \Vert _2^2\) is minimized at

\[ \alpha ^* = \frac {b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp }, \qquad \mathcal {R}(\alpha ^*) = v_\parallel + \frac {v_\perp b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp } < v_\parallel + v_\perp = \mathcal {R}(1). \]

Under isotropic Gaussian noise in orthogonal dimension \(M - r \ge 3\), positive-part James–Stein shrinkage dominates \(\alpha = 1\) uniformly ([JS61], Efron & Morris [EM73]); baux.stein applies the empirical moment plug-in \(\hat {\alpha } = \Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2 / (\Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2 + \hat {v}_\perp )\) for any \(M - r \ge 1\). When held-out validation pairs are available, baux.calibrate fits an affine variance rescaling and residual quantile multiplier to posterior standard errors.

Below, a PyTree domain combining logarithmic gain, periodic phase, simplex filter weights, and a categorical window drives a noisy 6-channel response concentrated near a rank-2 frame:

import math
import numpy as np
import baux

rng = np.random.default_rng(3)
space = {
    "gain": baux.Log(1e-3, 1.0),
    "phase": baux.Periodic(0.0, 2.0 * math.pi),
    "weights": baux.Simplex(4),
    "window": baux.categorical(["hann", "hamming", "blackman", "kaiser"]),
}
loadings = np.array([
    [1.0, 0.0],
    [0.8, 0.2],
    [0.2, 0.9],
    [-0.4, 0.7],
    [0.5, -0.5],
    [-0.3, -0.6],
])
frame = baux.Stiefel(loadings)
target_w = np.array([0.45, 0.30, 0.15, 0.10])
win_loss = {"hann": 0.0, "hamming": 0.04, "blackman": 0.08, "kaiser": 0.01}


def spectral_channels(p):
    g_err = math.log10(p["gain"] / 0.05)
    ph_err = math.sin(0.5 * (p["phase"] - 1.2))
    w_err = float(np.sum((np.sqrt(p["weights"]) - np.sqrt(target_w)) ** 2))
    latent = np.array([-(g_err**2 + w_err + win_loss[p["window"]]), -(ph_err**2)])
    return loadings @ latent + 0.15 * rng.normal(size=6)


pipe = (
    baux.enn(k=8, geometry="conformal")
    | baux.stein(frame)
    | baux.ucb(beta=1.5)
    | baux.regions(count=3)
)
state = baux.init(space, pipe, key=baux.key(19))
for _ in range(10):
    state, batch = baux.ask(state, num_arms=3)
    y = np.vstack([spectral_channels(p) for p in batch.params])
    state, mu_shrunk = baux.tell(state, batch, y)

assert mu_shrunk.shape == (3, 6) and state.y_obs.shape == (30, 6)

4. Closed-Loop Control Experiments

Closed-loop tokamak magnetic and profile control problems (Degrave et al. [Deg+22], Tracey et al. [Tra+24], Pfau et al. [Pfa+25]) test direct parameter-space policy search when neural network weights (\(D \sim 1.5 \times 10^5\)) are evaluated on full trajectory rollouts. On the toka_gym TCV plasma rampdown benchmark (Wang et al. [Wan+25], Theiler et al. [The+26]), a four-layer policy (\(D = 136{,}712\) weights) is searched over \(100\) sequential rounds (\(n = 10\) paired seeds). Multi-region ENN-TuRBO (\(K = 10\), \(R = 10\)) reduces tracking error from 65.72 at initialization to 1.68 (origin_only) and 1.79 (ellipsoid_ball), while on-policy PPO reaches 20.59 at the same round budget.

Over a \(10{,}000\)-round horizon in TORAX 2.0.0 (Citrin et al. [Cit+24]), a \(93 \to 256 \to 256 \to 256 \to 19\) policy (\(D = 160{,}531\) weights) couples four 1D radial transport PDEs with Miller local-equilibrium shaping (Miller et al. [Mil+98]). Running surrogate-free TuRBO-zero (\(R = 10\), \(n = 10\) paired seeds) to compare cross-region incumbent sharing rules directly, circumscribing-ball adoption (ellipsoid_ball) reaches 91.57 ± 0.55 and independent regions (origin_only) reach 89.41 ± 0.97 (paired difference +2.16 ± 0.91; two-sided paired \(t_9\) \(p = 0.0410\) unadjusted, \(p_{\mathrm {Holm}} = 0.246\) across six checkpoints; PPO reaches 44.25). Exported 3D tokamak equilibria from FUSE.jl (Meneghini et al. [Men+24]), 3D stratified fluid fields from Oceananigans.jl (Ramadhan et al. [Ram+20]), and rollout trajectories can be inspected in Simulation.
