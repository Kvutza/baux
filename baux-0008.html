Product Manifolds & Subspace Contraction

1. Section–Retraction Charts on Product Spaces

Structured parameter spaces factor as products \(\mathcal {M} = \prod _{\ell =1}^L \mathcal {M}_\ell \) across multiplicative scales, periodic phases, probability simplices, and discrete sets. Each factor \(\mathcal {M}_\ell \) is equipped with a section \(\psi _\ell : \mathcal {M}_\ell \to \mathcal {U}_\ell \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{d_\ell }\) into flat solver coordinates and a retraction \(\rho _\ell : \mathcal {U}_\ell \to \mathcal {M}_\ell \) satisfying \(\rho _\ell \circ \psi _\ell = \mathrm {id}_{\mathcal {M}_\ell }\), so neighbor search and trust-region perturbations operate in \(\mathcal {U} = \prod _\ell \mathcal {U}_\ell \) while evaluations retract cleanly onto \(\mathcal {M}\):

\[ \begin {CD} \mathcal {M}_\ell @>{\psi _\ell }>> \mathcal {U}_\ell \\ @V{\mathrm {id}_{\mathcal {M}_\ell }}VV @VV{\rho _\ell }V \\ \mathcal {M}_\ell @= \mathcal {M}_\ell \end {CD} \]

Euclidean distance on \(\mathcal {U}_\ell \) pulls back to intrinsic geometry on each factor:

  • Multiplicative intervals (baux.Log): On \([a, b] \subset \mathbb {R}_+\), the logarithmic section and exponential retraction \[ \psi (x) = \frac {\log x - \log a}{\log b - \log a} \in [0, 1], \qquad \rho (u) = a\left (\frac {b}{a}\right )^{\Pi _{[0,1]}(u)} \] pull back Euclidean distance to the scale-invariant metric \(ds^2 = \tfrac {dx^2}{x^2}\) up to \(\bigl (\log b - \log a\bigr )^{-2}\).
  • Circular phases (baux.Periodic): On \(\lbrack a, b\rparen \cong S^1\) of period \(P = b - a\) with phase \(\phi (x) = \tfrac {2\pi (x - a)}{P}\), the unit-circumference embedding and \(\operatorname {atan2}\) retraction \[ \psi (x) = \left (\frac {1}{2} + \frac {\cos \phi (x)}{2\pi },\; \frac {1}{2} + \frac {\sin \phi (x)}{2\pi }\right ), \qquad d_{\mathcal {U}}\bigl (\psi (x), \psi (x')\bigr ) = \frac {1}{\pi }\left |\sin \left (\frac {\phi (x) - \phi (x')}{2}\right )\right | \] induce chordal distance on \(S^1\) without boundary-wrap discontinuities.
  • Probability simplices (baux.Simplex): On \(\Delta ^{d-1}\), the positive-orthant square-root section and projective retraction \[ \psi (p) = \bigl (\sqrt {p_1}, \dots , \sqrt {p_d}\bigr ) \in S_+^{d-1}, \qquad \rho (u) = \frac {\max (u, 0)^{\odot 2}}{\Vert \max (u, 0)\Vert _2^2} \] pull back the ambient metric to \(\tfrac {1}{4}\,g_{\mathrm {FR}}\) on \(\operatorname {int}(\Delta ^{d-1})\), with squared chordal distance matching squared Hellinger distance \(\sum _{j=1}^d \bigl (\sqrt {p_j} - \sqrt {q_j}\bigr )^2\).
  • Constant-norm discrete codebooks (baux.categorical): Finite sets \(\{1, \dots , C\}\) embed into \(\mathbb {R}^{d_c}\) via constant-norm orthogonal or Rademacher codewords \(\psi (c)\), reducing nearest-codeword retraction \(\rho (u) = \arg \min _c \Vert u - \psi (c)\Vert _2\) to maximum inner-product decoding \(\arg \max _c \langle u, \psi (c)\rangle \).

2. Stiefel Signal-Subspace Contraction

When evaluations return correlated multi-output vectors \(y = \theta + \eta \in \mathbb {R}^M\) concentrated near a low-rank signal subspace, a loading matrix \(L \in \mathbb {R}^{M \times r}\) (\(1 \le r < M\)) orthonormalizes onto the Stiefel manifold \(V = L(L^\top L)^{-\frac {1}{2}} \in \mathrm {St}(r, M)\) (baux.Stiefel) with orthogonal complement \(V_\perp \in \mathrm {St}(M-r, M)\). Under \(P \eqdef (V^\top ,\, V_\perp ^\top )^\top : \mathbb {R}^M \xrightarrow {\cong } \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r}\), subspace contraction leaves tangent coordinates \(z_\parallel = V^\top y\) intact while shrinking normal coordinates \(z_\perp = V_\perp ^\top y\) (Stein [Ste56], James & Stein [JS61]):

\[ \begin {CD} \mathbb {R}^M @>{P}>> \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r} \\ @V{\hat {T}_{\alpha }}VV @VV{I_r \oplus \alpha I_{M-r}}V \\ \mathbb {R}^M @<<{P^\top }< \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r} \end {CD} \]

With zero-mean noise variances \(v_\parallel \eqdef \mathbb {E}\Vert V^\top \eta \Vert _2^2\) and \(v_\perp \eqdef \mathbb {E}\Vert V_\perp ^\top \eta \Vert _2^2 > 0\) and off-subspace signal energy \(b_\perp ^2 \eqdef \Vert V_\perp ^\top \theta \Vert _2^2\), the linear class \(\hat {\theta }_\alpha = V z_\parallel + \alpha V_\perp z_\perp \) attains its minimum quadratic risk \(\mathcal {R}(\alpha ) = \mathbb {E}\Vert \hat {\theta }_\alpha - \theta \Vert _2^2\) at the oracle contraction factor \(\alpha ^*\), strictly improving upon unshrunk estimation:

\[ \alpha ^* = \frac {b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp }, \qquad \mathcal {R}(\alpha ^*) = v_\parallel + \frac {v_\perp b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp } < v_\parallel + v_\perp = \mathcal {R}(1). \]

Under isotropic Gaussian noise in normal codimension \(M - r \ge 3\), positive-part James–Stein shrinkage uniformly dominates \(\alpha = 1\) ([JS61], Efron & Morris [EM73]), whereas baux.stein applies the empirical moment-ratio plug-in across any \(M - r \ge 1\):

\[ \hat {\alpha } = \frac {\Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2}{\Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2 + \hat {v}_\perp }. \]

When held-out calibration pairs are available, baux.calibrate rescales predictive dispersion via affine weighted least squares and standardized residual quantiles, yielding finite-sample coverage on exchangeable splits and an empirical dispersion adjustment on adaptive optimization histories where sequential selection breaks exchangeability.

Below, a PyTree product domain combining logarithmic gain, periodic phase, simplex filter weights, and categorical windowing drives a noisy 6-channel spectral response concentrated near a rank-2 Stiefel frame:

import math
import numpy as np
import baux

rng = np.random.default_rng(3)
space = {
    "gain": baux.Log(1e-3, 1.0),
    "phase": baux.Periodic(0.0, 2.0 * math.pi),
    "weights": baux.Simplex(4),
    "window": baux.categorical(["hann", "hamming", "blackman", "kaiser"]),
}
loadings = np.array([
    [1.0, 0.0],
    [0.8, 0.2],
    [0.2, 0.9],
    [-0.4, 0.7],
    [0.5, -0.5],
    [-0.3, -0.6],
])
frame = baux.Stiefel(loadings)
target_w = np.array([0.45, 0.30, 0.15, 0.10])
win_loss = {"hann": 0.0, "hamming": 0.04, "blackman": 0.08, "kaiser": 0.01}


def spectral_channels(p):
    g_err = math.log10(p["gain"] / 0.05)
    ph_err = math.sin(0.5 * (p["phase"] - 1.2))
    w_err = float(np.sum((np.sqrt(p["weights"]) - np.sqrt(target_w)) ** 2))
    latent = np.array([-(g_err**2 + w_err + win_loss[p["window"]]), -(ph_err**2)])
    return loadings @ latent + 0.15 * rng.normal(size=6)


pipe = (
    baux.enn(k=8, geometry="conformal")
    | baux.stein(frame)
    | baux.ucb(beta=1.5)
    | baux.regions(count=3)
)
state = baux.init(space, pipe, key=baux.key(19))
for _ in range(10):
    state, batch = baux.ask(state, num_arms=3)
    y = np.vstack([spectral_channels(p) for p in batch.params])
    state, mu_shrunk = baux.tell(state, batch, y)

assert mu_shrunk.shape == (3, 6) and state.y_obs.shape == (30, 6)
