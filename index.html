Baux

baux is a black box optimization library built around Epistemic Nearest Neighbors (Bafna et al. [BJS25]) and trust-region search.

1. Baseline & Lineage

The surrogate and trust-region loop follow Bafna, Jadhav, & Sweet [BJS25] (Taking the GP Out of the Loop) and yubo-research/enn, replacing Gaussian Process matrix inversion with inverse-variance nearest-neighbor smoothing (Aitken [Ait35], Cochran [Coc54]). baux extends this surrogate across non-Euclidean domain charts \(\mathcal {M}\) and multi-region portfolios.

Exact Gaussian Process inference over \(N\) observations inverts an \(N \times N\) Gram matrix in \(\mathcal {O}(N^3)\) time. Bafna et al. [BJS25] instead treat each observation as a local location–variance estimate and combine the \(K\) nearest neighbors of a query in \(\mathcal {O}(N D + N \log N)\) exact flat-scan time (or \(\mathcal {O}(N D + N \log K)\) with bounded top-\(K\) selection) on \(\mathbb {R}^D\).

Local-scale dissimilarities replace stationary lengthscales with conformal radius normalization (Zelnik-Manor & Perona [ZP04]), Poincaré half-space chordal invariants (Cannon et al. [CFK+97], Nickel & Kiela [NK17], Kisfaludi-Bak & van Wordragen [KvW24]), and a hybrid conformal–transport cost motivated by optimal transport (Villani [Vil09], Peyré & Cuturi [PC19]).

Product spaces \(\mathcal {M} = \prod _\ell \mathcal {M}_\ell \) map to solver coordinates via section–retraction charts \(\psi _\ell : \mathcal {M}_\ell \to \mathcal {U}_\ell \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{d_\ell }\) and \(\rho _\ell : \mathcal {U}_\ell \to \mathcal {M}_\ell \) covering positive scales (Čencov [Cen82]), circular phases, probability simplices in Bhattacharyya coordinates [Bha43], and discrete codebooks via one-hot or Rademacher embeddings (Johnson & Lindenstrauss [JL84], Achlioptas [Ach03]). Multi-output codomains support polar Stiefel projection (Higham [Hig86]), orthogonal subspace shrinkage (Stein [Ste56], James & Stein [JS61]), and split-conformal residual scaling (Papadopoulos et al. [Pap+02], Vovk et al. [VGS05], Lei et al. [LGR+18]).

Trust-region search (Eriksson et al. [EPG+19]) adapts bounding side lengths from consecutive improvement and failure counters, perturbs axis-aligned coordinate subsets (RAASP [EPG+19], Rashidi et al. [RJG24]), and coordinates multi-region portfolios (Daulton et al. [DEB+22], [DBB20]) through epoch-guarded ledgers and augmented Chebyshev scalarizations (Knowles [Kno06], Paria et al. [PKP20]).

The runtime represents optimizer state as an immutable snapshot paired with a splittable 128-bit PRNG key (Claessen & Pałka [CP13], Steele et al. [SLF14]), folding rollouts via scan and executing distance and candidate kernels on portable SIMD CPU, Metal, and CUDA backends.

2. Theoretical Foundations

2.1. Precision-Weighted Nearest Neighbors

Let \(\mathcal {X} \subseteq \mathbb {R}^D\) denote the coordinate domain, and let \(\mathcal {D} = \{(x_m, y_m, s_m)\}_{m=1}^N \subset \mathcal {X} \times \mathbb {R} \times \mathbb {R}_+\) denote \(N\) evaluations with observation noise \(y_m = f(x_m) + s_m \varepsilon _m\), where \(\varepsilon _m \stackrel {\text {i.i.d.}}{\sim } \mathcal {N}(0, 1)\). Rather than inverting an \(N \times N\) covariance matrix in \(\mathcal {O}(N^3)\) time, Epistemic Nearest Neighbors (Bafna et al. [BJS25]) treats each observation as a local working estimate and combines its \(K\) nearest neighbors by inverse-variance weighting (Aitken [Ait35], Cochran [Coc54]). Given a nonnegative pairwise dissimilarity \(\delta : \mathcal {X} \times \mathcal {X} \to \mathbb {R}_+\), empirical target variance scale \(\hat {\sigma }_y^2 > 0\), and dimensionless scale parameters \(\theta = (s_0^2, c_e) \in \mathbb {R}_+ \times \mathbb {R}_+\), observation \(m\) is assigned the working moments

\[ \begin {aligned} \mu (x \mid x_m, y_m, s_m) &= y_m, \\ \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_m, y_m, s_m) &= \hat {\sigma }_y^2 s_0^2 + s_m^2, \\ \sigma _e^2(x \mid x_m, y_m, s_m) &= \hat {\sigma }_y^2 c_e\, \delta (x, x_m). \end {aligned} \]

Let \(\pi _1(x), \dots , \pi _K(x) \in \{1, \dots , N\}\) index the \(K\) nearest neighbors of \(x\) ordered by nondecreasing \(\delta (x, x_{\pi _i(x)})\) (breaking ties by smaller datum index), and write \(N_K(x) \eqdef \{\pi _1(x), \dots , \pi _K(x)\}\). With variance floor \(\varepsilon _{\mathrm {var}} = 10^{-9} \hat {\sigma }_y^2\) preventing division by zero when \(x = x_m\) and \(s_0 = s_m = 0\), the precision weights \(w_i(x) \eqdef \bigl (\varepsilon _{\mathrm {var}} + \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)}) + \sigma _e^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)})\bigr )^{-1}\) define a deterministic local smoothing surrogate evaluated in \(\mathcal {O}(N D + N \log N)\) exact flat-scan time per query:

\[ \begin {aligned} \mu (x) &= \frac {\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)\, y_{\pi _i(x)}}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \\[6pt] \sigma _a^2(x) &= \frac {\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)\, \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)}, y_{\pi _i(x)}, s_{\pi _i(x)})}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \\[6pt] \sigma _e^2(x) &= \frac {1}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \qquad \sigma ^2(x) \eqdef \sigma _e^2(x) + \sigma _a^2(x). \end {aligned} \]

Here \(\sigma _e^2(x)\) is a distance-sensitive dispersion proxy rather than a joint Gaussian posterior variance: under independent neighbor errors of variances \(v_i = \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)})\), the noise variance of the linear combination \(\mu (x)\) is \(\sum _{i=1}^K w_i^2(x) v_i / (\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x))^2\). When observations are well-separated relative to observation noise and \(f\) satisfies a local envelope \(|f(x_{\pi _i(x)}) - f(x)| \le \hat {\sigma }_y \sqrt {c_e\,\delta (x, x_{\pi _i(x)})}\), including distant neighbors increases worst-case squared bias faster than it reduces noise variance. Motivated by adaptive bandwidth selection (Lepski [Lep91]), the optional RiskPrefix rule scores each prefix \(r \in \{1, \dots , K\}\) by

\[ \mathcal {R}(r; x) \eqdef \hat {\sigma }_y^2 c_e\, \delta (x, x_{\pi _r(x)}) + \frac {\sum _{i=1}^r w_i^2(x)\, \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)}, y_{\pi _i(x)}, s_{\pi _i(x)})}{\bigl (\sum _{i=1}^r w_i(x)\bigr )^2}, \]

and truncates the neighbor prefix at \(r^*(x) \eqdef \arg \min _{1 \le r \le K} \mathcal {R}(r; x)\), whereas the default ENN configuration retains the full \(K\)-neighbor prefix.

2.2. Pseudolikelihood Estimation & Coherent Function Draws

Hyperparameters \(\theta = (s_0^2, c_e)\) are selected by maximizing a subsampled leave-one-out (LOO) Gaussian log-pseudolikelihood over \(S_P \subset \{1, \dots , N\}\) of size \(P \le N\) (Bafna et al. [BJS25]):

\[ \begin {aligned} \widehat {\log \mathcal {L}}(\theta ) &\eqdef \frac {1}{P} \sum _{n \in S_P} \ell _{-n}(\theta ), \\[4pt] \ell _{-n}(\theta ) &\eqdef -\frac {1}{2}\left [\log \bigl (2\pi \sigma _{-n}^2(x_n; \theta )\bigr ) + \frac {\bigl (y_n - \mu _{-n}(x_n; \theta )\bigr )^2}{\sigma _{-n}^2(x_n; \theta )}\right ], \end {aligned} \]

where \(\mu _{-n}(x_n; \theta )\) and \(\sigma _{-n}^2(x_n; \theta ) \eqdef \sigma _{e,-n}^2(x_n; \theta ) + \sigma _{a,-n}^2(x_n; \theta )\) exclude datum \(n\) from its own \(K\)-neighbor set. Candidate pairs are drawn by log-uniform Monte Carlo sampling from \(c_e \bar {\delta } \in [10^{-3}, 10^3]\) and \(s_0^2 \in [10^{-6}, 1]\) (together with optional warm-start candidates), where \(\bar {\delta }\) denotes the mean positive off-diagonal dissimilarity in the optimizer's internal LOO fitter and \(\bar {\delta } = 1\) on unit-scaled standalone enn_fit states.

Proposition 2.1 (Coherent Function Draws). Fix seed \(s\) and output metric \(j\), and associate an independent standard normal variable \(z_{s,m,j} \stackrel {\text {i.i.d.}}{\sim } \mathcal {N}(0, 1)\) with each global datum index \(m \in \{1, \dots , N\}\). For query \(x \in \mathcal {X}\) with ordered neighbor indices \(\pi _1(x), \dots , \pi _K(x)\), let \(\bar {w}_{i,j}(x) \eqdef w_{i,j}(x) / \sum _{r=1}^K w_{r,j}(x)\) and write \(\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x) \eqdef \bar {w}_{i,j}(x)\) when \(m = \pi _i(x) \in N_K(x)\) (and \(0\) otherwise). Define the latent function draw

\[ \tilde {f}_{s,j}(x) \eqdef \mu _j(x) + \frac {\sigma _{e,j}(x)}{\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2} \sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}(x)\, z_{s,\pi _i(x),j}, \qquad \Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2 \eqdef \Bigl (\sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}^2(x)\Bigr )^{1/2}. \]

(Replacing \(\sigma _{e,j}(x)\) by \(\sigma _j(x)\) yields noisy observation draws.) Then for every \(x \in \mathcal {X}\), \(\tilde {f}_{s,j}(x) \sim \mathcal {N}\bigl (\mu _j(x),\, \sigma _{e,j}^2(x)\bigr )\), trajectories are smooth within the interior of each order-\(K\) Voronoi cell where \(N_K(x)\) is constant (with step transitions across Voronoi boundaries), and any two queries \(x, x' \in \mathcal {X}\) sharing neighbors \(S_K(x, x') \eqdef N_K(x) \cap N_K(x') \ne \emptyset \) satisfy

\[ \operatorname {Cov}\bigl (\tilde {f}_{s,j}(x),\, \tilde {f}_{s,j}(x')\bigr ) = \frac {\sigma _{e,j}(x)\,\sigma _{e,j}(x')}{\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2\,\Vert \bar {w}_j(x')\Vert _2} \sum _{m \in S_K(x, x')} \tilde {w}_{m,j}(x)\,\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x') > 0. \]

Proof. By linearity of expectation, \(\mathbb {E}[\tilde {f}_{s,j}(x)] = \mu _j(x)\). Because \(\pi _1(x), \dots , \pi _K(x)\) are distinct global indices in \(\{1, \dots , N\}\), the variables \(z_{s,\pi _1(x),j}, \dots , z_{s,\pi _K(x),j}\) are independent standard Gaussians, so \(\xi _j(x) \eqdef \sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}(x)\, z_{s,\pi _i(x),j} = \sum _{m=1}^N \tilde {w}_{m,j}(x)\, z_{s,m,j}\) is Gaussian with variance \(\sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}^2(x) = \Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2^2 > 0\). Scaling \(\xi _j(x)\) by \(\sigma _{e,j}(x) / \Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2\) yields marginal variance

\[ \operatorname {Var}\bigl (\tilde {f}_{s,j}(x)\bigr ) = \left (\frac {\sigma _{e,j}(x)}{\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2}\right )^2 \Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2^2 = \sigma _{e,j}^2(x). \]

For any pair \(x, x'\), bilinearity and \(\mathbb {E}[z_{s,m,j}\, z_{s,m',j}] = \delta _{m,m'}\) over global datum indices give

\[ \operatorname {Cov}\bigl (\xi _j(x),\, \xi _j(x')\bigr ) = \sum _{m=1}^N \sum _{m'=1}^N \tilde {w}_{m,j}(x)\,\tilde {w}_{m',j}(x')\,\delta _{m,m'} = \sum _{m \in S_K(x, x')} \tilde {w}_{m,j}(x)\,\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x'). \]

Since \(\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x) > 0\) and \(\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x') > 0\) for every \(m \in S_K(x, x')\) and \(S_K(x, x') \ne \emptyset \), the sum is strictly positive.\(\;\square \)

2.3. Local-Scale Dissimilarities

Let \(d(x, x') \eqdef \Vert x - x'\Vert _2\) denote Euclidean (or coordinate-scaled Euclidean) distance. For local scale order \(k_0 \ge 1\) and floor \(\underline {\sigma } = 10^{-6}\), let \(\sigma (x) \eqdef \max \bigl (d(x, x_{(k_0)}),\, \underline {\sigma }\bigr )\) denote the \(k_0\)-th positive Euclidean neighbor radius at \(x\) (excluding zero-distance self-matches). Because \(\sigma (x)\) depends on the empirical configuration \(\mathcal {D}\), the resulting transformations are symmetric nonnegative dissimilarities rather than triangle-inequality metrics:

Under conformal normalization (Zelnik-Manor & Perona [ZP04]), squared distance is normalized by the product of endpoint radii, yielding a dimensionless ratio:

\[ \delta _c(x, x_m) \eqdef \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{\sigma (x)\, \sigma (x_m)}. \]

Under the Poincaré half-space dissimilarity (Cannon et al. [CFK+97], Nickel & Kiela [NK17], Kisfaludi-Bak & van Wordragen [KvW24]), points lift into the upper half-space \(\mathbb {H}(\mathcal {X}) \eqdef \mathcal {X} \times \mathbb {R}_+\) with metric \(ds^2 = (\Vert dx\Vert _2^2 + d\sigma ^2)/\sigma ^2\) via \(\Lambda (x) \eqdef (x, \sigma (x))\):

\[ \begin {CD} \mathcal {X} \times \mathcal {X} @>{\Lambda \times \Lambda }>> \mathbb {H}(\mathcal {X}) \times \mathbb {H}(\mathcal {X}) \\ @V{\delta _h}VV @VV{\cosh d_{\mathbb {H}} - 1}V \\ \mathbb {R}_+ @= \mathbb {R}_+ \end {CD} \]

The isometric invariant \(\cosh d_{\mathbb {H}}\bigl (\Lambda (x), \Lambda (x_m)\bigr ) - 1\) decomposes into conformal horizontal separation and dimensionless vertical scale mismatch:

\[ \delta _h(x, x_m) \eqdef \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{2\,\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)} + \frac {\bigl (\sigma (x) - \sigma (x_m)\bigr )^2}{2\,\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)}. \]

Under the hybrid conformal–transport dissimilarity, motivated by Gaussian optimal transport (Villani [Vil09], Peyré & Cuturi [PC19]), geometric-mean scale normalization is paired additively with the 1D squared radius transport penalty \((\sigma (x) - \sigma (x_m))^2\):

\[ \delta _w(x, x_m) \eqdef \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{\sqrt {\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)}} + \bigl (\sigma (x) - \sigma (x_m)\bigr )^2. \]

3. Domain Charts & Codomain Operators

3.1. Section–Retraction Manifold Charts

Search spaces decompose as products \(\mathcal {M} = \prod _{\ell =1}^L \mathcal {M}_\ell \) of coordinate factors: positive intervals \([a, b] \subset \mathbb {R}_+\), periodic intervals \(\lbrack a, b\rparen \cong S^1\), probability simplices \(\Delta ^{d-1}\), and finite categorical sets. Each factor \(\mathcal {M}_\ell \) carries an embedding \(\psi _\ell : \mathcal {M}_\ell \to \mathcal {U}_\ell \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{d_\ell }\) and retraction \(\rho _\ell : \mathcal {U}_\ell \to \mathcal {M}_\ell \) satisfying the left-inverse identity \(\rho _\ell \circ \psi _\ell = \mathrm {id}_{\mathcal {M}_\ell }\) (deterministically for Log, Periodic, Simplex, and one-hot Categorical, and whenever Rademacher codewords are distinct):

\[ \begin {CD} \mathcal {M}_\ell @>{\psi _\ell }>> \mathcal {U}_\ell \\ @V{\mathrm {id}_{\mathcal {M}_\ell }}VV @VV{\rho _\ell }V \\ \mathcal {M}_\ell @= \mathcal {M}_\ell \end {CD} \]

Proposition 3.1 (Chart Metric Properties). The factor charts \((\psi _\ell , \rho _\ell )\) satisfy:

1. Multiplicative positive reals (Čencov [Cen82]): For \(0 < a < b < \infty \) on \([a, b]\) equipped with the scale-invariant metric \(ds^2 = (dx / x)^2\), the logarithmic chart

\[ \psi (x) \eqdef \frac {\log (x / a)}{\log (b / a)} \in [0, 1], \qquad \rho (u) \eqdef a \left (\frac {b}{a}\right )^{\Pi _{[0,1]}(u)}, \]

satisfies \(|\psi (x) - \psi (x')| = \frac {1}{\log (b/a)}|\log (x/x')|\), an exact isometry up to constant factor \(\log (b / a)\).

2. Periodic angular phases: For period \(P = b - a > 0\) on \(\lbrack a, b\rparen \cong S^1\) with phase \(\phi (x) \eqdef 2\pi (x - a)/P \in \lbrack 0, 2\pi \rparen \), the circular trigonometric embedding maps \(S^1\) onto the circle of radius \((2\pi )^{-1}\) centered at \((\tfrac {1}{2}, \tfrac {1}{2}) \in [0, 1]^2\):

\[ \begin {aligned} \psi (x) &\eqdef \left (\frac {1}{2} + \frac {\cos \phi (x)}{2\pi },\, \frac {1}{2} + \frac {\sin \phi (x)}{2\pi }\right ), \\ \rho (u_1, u_2) &\eqdef a + \frac {P}{2\pi }\Bigl (\operatorname {atan2}\bigl (u_2 - \tfrac {1}{2},\, u_1 - \tfrac {1}{2}\bigr ) \bmod 2\pi \Bigr ), \end {aligned} \]

with \(\rho \) smooth on \(\mathbb {R}^2 \setminus \{(\tfrac {1}{2}, \tfrac {1}{2})\}\). The embedded curve has unit arc-length circumference, and its Euclidean chord distance \(\Vert \psi (x) - \psi (x')\Vert _2 = \frac {1}{\pi }\bigl |\sin (\Delta \phi / 2)\bigr |\) is strictly increasing in shortest circular angle \(\Delta \phi \in [0, \pi ]\).

3. Probability simplex (Bhattacharyya [Bha43]): On \(\Delta ^{d-1} \eqdef \{p \in \mathbb {R}_+^d : \sum _{j=1}^d p_j = 1\}\) with Fisher–Rao metric \(g_{\mathrm {FR}} = \sum _{j=1}^d (dp_j)^2 / p_j\), the positive-orthant square-root map and clamped retraction (for \(\max (u, 0) \ne 0\))

\[ \psi (p) \eqdef \bigl (\sqrt {p_1}, \dots , \sqrt {p_d}\bigr ) \in S_+^{d-1}, \qquad \rho (u) \eqdef \frac {\max (u, 0)^{\odot 2}}{\Vert \max (u, 0)\Vert _2^2}, \]

satisfy \(\psi ^* g_{\mathbb {R}^d} = \frac {1}{4}\, g_{\mathrm {FR}}\) on the interior of \(\Delta ^{d-1}\), while squared chord distance gives the squared Hellinger distance:

\[ \Vert \psi (p) - \psi (q)\Vert _2^2 = \sum _{j=1}^d \bigl (\sqrt {p_j} - \sqrt {q_j}\bigr )^2 = 2 \left (1 - \sum _{j=1}^d \sqrt {p_j q_j}\right ). \]

4. Discrete categorical codebooks: For \(C\) categories, encoding="auto" embeds small sets (\(C \le 15\)) via scaled one-hot vectors in \(\mathbb {R}^C\) (pairwise distance \(\sqrt {2}\,s\) for \(c \ne c'\), \(s = 10\)), and larger sets into \(\mathbb {R}^{d_c}\) (\(d_c = \lceil 8 \ln C \rceil \)) via Rademacher codewords \(\psi (c) \in \{-s/\sqrt {d_c},\, +s/\sqrt {d_c}\}^{d_c}\) (Johnson & Lindenstrauss [JL84], Achlioptas [Ach03]), retracting by nearest codeword:

\[ \rho (u) \eqdef \arg \min _{c \in \{1, \dots , C\}} \Vert u - \psi (c)\Vert _2. \]

Proof. On \([a, b]\), \(d\psi = dx / (x \log (b/a))\), so \((d\psi )^2 = (dx / x)^2 / \log ^2(b/a)\). On \(\lbrack a, b\rparen \), \(\Vert \psi (x) - \psi (x')\Vert _2^2 = \frac {1}{4\pi ^2}\bigl ((\cos \phi - \cos \phi ')^2 + (\sin \phi - \sin \phi ')^2\bigr ) = \frac {1}{2\pi ^2}(1 - \cos \Delta \phi ) = \frac {1}{\pi ^2}\sin ^2(\Delta \phi / 2)\), which is strictly increasing for \(\Delta \phi \in [0, \pi ]\). On \(\mathrm {int}(\Delta ^{d-1})\), \(d\psi _j = dp_j / (2\sqrt {p_j})\), whence \(\sum _{j=1}^d (d\psi _j)^2 = \frac {1}{4}\sum _{j=1}^d (dp_j)^2 / p_j\), and expanding \(\sum _{j=1}^d (\sqrt {p_j} - \sqrt {q_j})^2\) with \(\sum _j p_j = \sum _j q_j = 1\) yields \(2(1 - \sum _j \sqrt {p_j q_j})\). Finally, since every one-hot or Rademacher codeword has constant Euclidean norm (\(\Vert \psi (c)\Vert _2^2 = s^2\)), \(\Vert u - \psi (c)\Vert _2^2 = \Vert u\Vert _2^2 + s^2 - 2\langle u, \psi (c)\rangle \), so nearest-neighbor retraction coincides with \(\arg \max _c \langle u, \psi (c)\rangle \) (breaking ties deterministically).\(\;\square \)

3.2. Codomain Shrinkage & Calibration

Given raw loading matrix \(L \in \mathbb {R}^{M \times r}\) (\(1 \le r < M\)) orthonormalized onto the Stiefel manifold \(V = L(L^\top L)^{-1/2} \in \mathrm {St}(r, M)\) via polar decomposition (Higham [Hig86]) with orthogonal complement \(V_\perp \in \mathrm {St}(M-r, M)\), the orthogonal isomorphism \(P \eqdef (V^\top ,\, V_\perp ^\top )^\top : \mathbb {R}^M \xrightarrow {\cong } \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r}\) factors codomain contraction (Stein [Ste56], James & Stein [JS61]):

\[ \begin {CD} \mathbb {R}^M @>{P}>> \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r} \\ @V{\hat {T}_{\alpha }}VV @VV{I_r \oplus \alpha I_{M-r}}V \\ \mathbb {R}^M @<<{P^\top }< \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r} \end {CD} \]

Theorem 3.2 (Oracle Linear-Bayes Subspace Dominance). Let \(y = \theta + \eta \in \mathbb {R}^M\) satisfy \(\mathbb {E}[\eta ] = 0\), and decompose \(y\) along \(V \in \mathrm {St}(r, M)\) (\(1 \le r < M\)) into \(z_\parallel \eqdef V^\top y \in \mathbb {R}^r\) with noise variance \(v_\parallel \eqdef \mathbb {E}\Vert V^\top \eta \Vert _2^2\) and normal coordinates \(z_\perp \eqdef V_\perp ^\top y \in \mathbb {R}^{M-r}\) with noise variance \(v_\perp \eqdef \mathbb {E}\Vert V_\perp ^\top \eta \Vert _2^2 > 0\) and squared orthogonal signal norm \(b_\perp ^2 \eqdef \Vert V_\perp ^\top \theta \Vert _2^2 \ge 0\). Over the linear class \(\hat {\theta }_\alpha \eqdef V z_\parallel + \alpha V_\perp z_\perp \) (\(\alpha \in \mathbb {R}\)), the oracle linear-Bayes weight \(\alpha ^* \eqdef b_\perp ^2 / (b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp )\) uniquely minimizes \(\mathcal {R}(\alpha ) \eqdef \mathbb {E}\Vert \hat {\theta }_\alpha - \theta \Vert _2^2\), achieving

\[ \mathcal {R}(\alpha ^*) = v_\parallel + \frac {v_\perp b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp } < \mathcal {R}(1) = v_\parallel + v_\perp . \]

Proof. By orthogonality of \(V\) and \(V_\perp \) and \(\mathbb {E}[z_\perp - V_\perp ^\top \theta ] = 0\),

\[ \mathcal {R}(\alpha ) = \mathbb {E}\Vert V^\top \eta \Vert _2^2 + \mathbb {E}\Vert \alpha V_\perp ^\top \eta - (1 - \alpha ) V_\perp ^\top \theta \Vert _2^2 = v_\parallel + \alpha ^2 v_\perp + (1 - \alpha )^2 b_\perp ^2. \]

Since \(v_\perp + b_\perp ^2 > 0\), \(\mathcal {R}(\alpha )\) is strictly convex with unique stationary point \(2\alpha v_\perp - 2(1 - \alpha )b_\perp ^2 = 0\), giving \(\alpha ^* = b_\perp ^2 / (b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp ) \in \lbrack 0, 1\rparen \). Substituting \(\alpha ^*\) gives

\[ \begin {aligned} \mathcal {R}(\alpha ^*) &= v_\parallel + \frac {b_\perp ^4 v_\perp + v_\perp ^2 b_\perp ^2}{(b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp )^2} = v_\parallel + \frac {v_\perp b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp }, \\[4pt] \mathcal {R}(1) - \mathcal {R}(\alpha ^*) &= v_\perp - \frac {v_\perp b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp } = \frac {v_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp } > 0.\;\square \end {aligned} \]

Because \(b_\perp ^2\) is unknown in practice, finite-sample dominance requires estimating \(\alpha ^*\). Under isotropic Gaussian noise \(\eta \sim \mathcal {N}(0, \sigma ^2 I_M)\) whenever the normal codimension satisfies \(M - r \ge 3\), the positive-part James–Stein weight \(\hat {\alpha }_{\mathrm {JS}} = \bigl (1 - (M - r - 2)\sigma ^2 / \Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2\bigr )_+\) uniformly dominates \(\alpha = 1\) in frequentist risk (James & Stein [JS61], Efron & Morris [EM73]); baux's online stein operator applies the moment-ratio plug-in \(\hat {\alpha } = \Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2 / (\Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2 + \hat {v}_\perp )\) across arbitrary codimension \(M - r \ge 1\).

Residual dispersion can be post-hoc rescaled via affine weighted least squares [Ait35] and split-conformal prediction (Papadopoulos et al. [Pap+02], Vovk et al. [VGS05], Lei et al. [LGR+18]). Given an independent held-out calibration split \(\mathcal {D}_{\mathrm {cal}} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_c}\) and miscoverage level \(\alpha \in \bigl \lbrack \frac {1}{N_c + 1},\, 1\bigr \rparen \), standardized scores \(E_i \eqdef |y_i - \hat {\mu }(x_i)| / \hat {\sigma }(x_i)\) define the order-statistic multiplier

\[ \begin {aligned} \hat {q}_{1-\alpha } &\eqdef E_{(\lceil (N_c + 1)(1 - \alpha )\rceil )}, \\ C_{1-\alpha }(x) &\eqdef \bigl [\hat {\mu }(x) - \hat {q}_{1-\alpha }\,\hat {\sigma }(x),\, \hat {\mu }(x) + \hat {q}_{1-\alpha }\,\hat {\sigma }(x)\bigr ]. \end {aligned} \]

(For \(\alpha < \frac {1}{N_c + 1}\), the implementation clamps the rank to \(N_c\), returning the empirical maximum residual; for alpha=None, it uses RMS residual scaling.) When \(\mathcal {D}_{\mathrm {cal}} \cup \{(x_{\mathrm {new}}, y_{\mathrm {new}})\}\) is exchangeable and disjoint from the surrogate training history, \(\mathbb {P}\bigl (y_{\mathrm {new}} \in C_{1-\alpha }(x_{\mathrm {new}})\bigr ) \ge 1 - \alpha \); on adaptive sequential optimization histories where exchangeability fails, \(\hat {q}_{1-\alpha }\) serves as an empirical scale heuristic rather than a distribution-free coverage guarantee.

4. Trust-Region Dynamics & Portfolio State

4.1. Trust-Region Adaptation & Candidate Sampling

Following TuRBO (Eriksson et al. [EPG+19]), let \(T(x_0, \ell ) \subset \mathcal {X}\) denote an axis-aligned trust region centered at incumbent \(x_0 \in \mathcal {X}\) with side length \(\ell \in [\underline {\ell }, \overline {\ell }]\). In solver coordinates \(u \in \mathcal {U} \subseteq \mathbb {R}^D\), component \(j\) spans \([u_{0,j} - \frac {1}{2}\ell _j,\, u_{0,j} + \frac {1}{2}\ell _j]\) clipped to domain bounds. Consecutive counters \(c_{\mathrm {succ}}, c_{\mathrm {fail}}\) start at zero; after evaluating a batch \(B\) with best value \(y^* \eqdef \max _{(x, y) \in B} y\) and relative improvement threshold \(\tau _y(x_0) \eqdef y(x_0) + 10^{-3}|y(x_0)|\), the state updates by

\[ (\ell ', c_{\mathrm {succ}}', c_{\mathrm {fail}}') = \begin {cases} (\min (2\ell , \overline {\ell }),\, 0,\, 0), & \text {if } y^* > \tau _y(x_0) \text { and } c_{\mathrm {succ}} + 1 \ge \tau _{\mathrm {succ}}, \\[4pt] (\ell ,\, c_{\mathrm {succ}} + 1,\, 0), & \text {if } y^* > \tau _y(x_0) \text { and } c_{\mathrm {succ}} + 1 < \tau _{\mathrm {succ}}, \\[4pt] (\ell / 2,\, 0,\, 0), & \text {if } y^* \le \tau _y(x_0) \text { and } c_{\mathrm {fail}} + 1 \ge \tau _{\mathrm {fail}}, \\[4pt] (\ell ,\, 0,\, c_{\mathrm {fail}} + 1), & \text {if } y^* \le \tau _y(x_0) \text { and } c_{\mathrm {fail}} + 1 < \tau _{\mathrm {fail}}. \end {cases} \]

Initial space-filling designs draw from Latin Hypercube sampling (McKay, Beckman, & Conover [MBC79]) or Sobol' sequences [Sob67]. Local candidate pools of size \(Q\) are generated via Random Axis-Aligned Subspace Perturbation (RAASP [EPG+19], [DEB+22]; cf. Rashidi et al. [RJG24]): the default ORIG driver perturbs an exact uniform subset of \(k = \min (D, \max (1, d_p))\) coordinates (or tied chart blocks) per candidate, while the FAST driver samples binomial masks with probability \(p = \min (1, d_p / D)\) clamped to at least one active coordinate. Two observation regimes are supported:

In the noise-free regime, \(T(x_0, \ell )\) centers at \(x_0 \eqdef \arg \max _i y_i\) and can select \(q\) arms by peeling non-dominated \((\mu (x_c), \sigma (x_c))\) skyline fronts across \(Q\) candidates in \(\mathcal {O}\bigl (Q(N D + N \log N) + q Q \log Q\bigr )\) time.

In the noisy regime, \(x_0 \eqdef \arg \max _{i \in I_K} \mu (x_i)\) centers at the highest smoothed mean over the \(K\) highest raw observations \(I_K\), and arms are selected by Upper Confidence Bound \(\arg \max _{x_c} [\mu (x_c) + \beta \sigma _e(x_c)]\) (Srinivas et al. [SKK+10]) or Thompson function draws \(\arg \max _{x_c} \tilde {f}_s(x_c)\) (Thompson [Tho33], Russo et al. [RVR+18]).

Whenever contraction drives \(\ell < \underline {\ell }\), single-region search resets \(\ell \leftarrow \ell _0\) and schedules a fresh Latin Hypercube warmup, while multi-region portfolios increment the region epoch and re-center \(x_{0,r}\) uniformly or by maximin distance over random candidates.

4.2. Multi-Region Portfolios & Scalarization

Concurrent trust regions \(\{T_r(x_{0,r}, \ell _r)\}_{r=1}^R\) (Daulton et al. [DEB+22]) coordinate asynchronous batches through an epoch-guarded ledger:

Under UCB1 arm allocation (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi, & Fischer [ACF02]), each initialized region's incumbent value is min–max normalized to \(\tilde {u}_r \in [0, 1]\) across active regions, and arms route to \(\arg \max _r \bigl [\tilde {u}_r + c\sqrt {\ln (\sum _{r'} \tilde {n}_{r'}) / \tilde {n}_r}\bigr ]\) where \(\tilde {n}_r = n_r + q_r\) counts completed plus in-flight evaluations. Each issued proposal carries a ticket \(\mathrm {AskId} = (r, g, a)\) stamped with region \(r\)'s current epoch \(g = g_r\); completions always append to the global dataset \(\mathcal {D}\), and update \(T_r\)'s side length if and only if \(g = g_r\).

Under regional sharing (sharing="ellipsoid_ball"), region \(r\) adopts a better global observation as its incumbent whenever it falls inside the Euclidean ball circumscribing \(T_r\)'s axis-aligned trust-region box around \(x_{0,r}\) (whereas sharing="origin_only" restricts incumbent updates to proposals originated by region \(r\)).

For multi-objective problems with \(M \ge 2\) outputs (MORBO [DEB+22]; cf. qEHVI [DBB20]), empirical observations are min–max normalized per objective to \(\tilde {y}_j \in [0, 1]\), and each region draws simplex weights \(w \sim \mathrm {Dirichlet}(\mathbf {1}_M)\) to maximize the ParEGO augmented Chebyshev scalarization (Knowles [Kno06], Paria et al. [PKP20]):

\[ s_w(\tilde {y}) \eqdef \min _{1 \le j \le M} \bigl (w_j\, \tilde {y}_j\bigr ) + \eta \sum _{j=1}^M w_j\, \tilde {y}_j, \qquad \eta = 0.05. \]

4.3. Functional State & Checkpoint Replay

Observation history \(\mathcal {D}\), regional ledgers \(\{T_r\}_{r=1}^R\), splittable 128-bit PRNG state \(k \in \mathcal {K}\) (Claessen & Pałka [CP13], Steele et al. [SLF14]), and compiled domain specification \(\mathrm {Spec}(\mathcal {M})\) form an immutable state space \(S\):

\[ \begin {aligned} S &\eqdef \mathcal {K} \times \mathcal {D} \times \prod _{r=1}^R T_r \times \mathrm {Spec}(\mathcal {M}), \\ \alpha _{\mathrm {ask}} &: S \times \mathbb {N} \to S \times \operatorname {List}(\mathcal {X}), \\ \alpha _{\mathrm {tell}} &: S \times \operatorname {List}(\mathcal {X} \times \mathbb {R}^M) \to S \times \mathbb {R}^{B \times M}. \end {aligned} \]

Note that \(\alpha _{\mathrm {ask}}\) and \(\alpha _{\mathrm {tell}}\) form a stateful Mealy protocol rather than commuting operations (appending observations changes the surrogate neighborhood and regional incumbents for subsequent proposals). Determinism instead comes from pure functional transitions and lossless binary serialization: for any pure round transition \(\Phi _f : S \to S\) induced by an objective \(f\) and template state \(s_0 \in S\) fixing \(\mathrm {Spec}(\mathcal {M})\), canonical snapshot encoding \(\mathrm {freeze} : S \to \mathcal {B}\) and decoding \(\mathrm {thaw}_{s_0} : \mathcal {B} \to S\) satisfy \(\mathrm {thaw}_{s_0} \circ \mathrm {freeze} = \mathrm {id}_S\), so serialized step execution \(\widehat {\Phi }_f \eqdef \mathrm {freeze} \circ \Phi _f \circ \mathrm {thaw}_{s_0}\) commutes strictly with in-memory rollout:

\[ \begin {CD} S @>{\Phi _f}>> S \\ @V{\mathrm {freeze}}VV @AA{\mathrm {thaw}_{s_0}}A \\ \mathcal {B} @>>{\widehat {\Phi }_f}> \mathcal {B} \end {CD} \]

Likewise, pmap splits a parent key \(k \mapsto (k_1, \dots , k_B)\) before dispatching worker threads, ensuring identical results regardless of thread scheduling.

5.1. PyTree & Non-Euclidean Spaces 

import math
import numpy as np
import baux

space = {
    "scale": baux.Log(1e-4, 1e-1),
    "phase": baux.Periodic(0.0, 2.0 * math.pi),
    "mixture": baux.Simplex(4),
    "kernel": baux.categorical(["matern", "rbf", "rational"]),
}
target = np.array([0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1])
penalty = {"matern": 0.0, "rbf": 0.05, "rational": 0.1}


def objective(p):
    scale_err = (math.log10(p["scale"]) + 2.0) ** 2
    phase_err = 1.0 - math.cos(p["phase"] - math.pi / 3.0)
    mix_err = float(np.sum((np.sqrt(p["mixture"]) - np.sqrt(target)) ** 2))
    return -(scale_err + phase_err + mix_err + penalty[p["kernel"]])


state = baux.init(space, baux.turbo_enn(), key=baux.key(0))
state, batch = baux.ask(state, num_arms=4)
y = np.array([objective(p) for p in batch.params])
state, mu = baux.tell(state, batch, y)
print(state.best_params, state.best_y)

6. Tokamak Control Benchmarks

We evaluate baux on two closed-loop tokamak policy-search benchmarks motivated by TCV magnetic control (Degrave et al. [Deg+22], Tracey et al. [Tra+24], Pfau et al. [Pfa+25]): an ENN-UCB surrogate benchmark on the open-source EPFL/MIT toka_gym rampdown simulator (Wang et al. [Wan+25], Theiler et al. [The+26]), and a 10,000-round regional-sharing ablation in TORAX 2.0.0 (Citrin et al. [Cit+24]).

6.1. TokaGym Rampdown Control with ENN-UCB

On the toka_gym TCV rampdown benchmark ([Wan+25], [The+26]), a four-layer policy \(\pi _\theta : \mathbb {R}^{11} \to [-1, 1]^8\) of hidden width \(256\) (\(D = 136{,}712\) parameters) is searched directly from closed-loop trajectories (\(20\) steps \(\times \, 4\) substeps). Multi-region ENN-TuRBO fits a \(K = 10\) Epistemic Nearest Neighbor surrogate by LOO pseudolikelihood with UCB acquisition across \(R = 10\) regions (incumbent-ucb1, raasp-fast with \(d_p = 13{,}671\), \(4\) candidates and \(1\) evaluated arm per round). Across \(n = 10\) paired seeds over \(100\) rounds, tracking error falls from 65.72 at initialization to 1.79 (ellipsoid-ball sharing) and 1.68 (origin-only) versus 20.59 for on-policy Metal PPO at matching round budget.

6.2. TORAX Core-Transport & Shaping Regional Ablation

Because EPFL's free-boundary Grad–Shafranov coil-circuit simulator FGE used in [Deg+22], [Tra+24], and [Pfa+25] is proprietary, we evaluate the \(93 \to 256 \to 256 \to 256 \to 19\) ELU+\(\tanh \) policy architecture (\(D = 160{,}531\) parameters) in open-source TORAX 2.0.0 [Cit+24]. Rather than solving free-boundary E/F/OH/FPS coil circuits from \(38\) magnetic probes and \(34\) flux loops, this benchmark evolves iterhybrid CHEASE/MEQ geometry (\(R_0 = 6.2\text { m}\), \(B_0 = 5.3\text { T}\), \(15\) warmup steps followed by \(T = 20\) controlled steps) coupled to four 1D radial transport PDEs for \(T_e(\hat {\rho })\), \(T_i(\hat {\rho })\), \(n_e(\hat {\rho })\), and \(\psi (\hat {\rho })\) over \(\hat {\rho } \in [0, 1]\), mapping \(93\) radial profile and shaping residuals to \(19\) normalized plasma current, heating, current-drive, pellet/gas fueling, and Miller local-equilibrium shaping (kappa, delta_u, delta_l, \(Z_{\mathrm {axis}}\); Miller et al. [Mil+98]) actuators:

\[ \begin {aligned} R(\hat {\rho }, \vartheta ) &= R_{\mathrm {geo}}(\hat {\rho }) + \hat {\rho }\,a_{\min }\cos \bigl (\vartheta + \arcsin (\delta (\hat {\rho },\vartheta ))\sin \vartheta \bigr ), \\[4pt] Z(\hat {\rho }, \vartheta ) &= Z_{\mathrm {axis}} + \kappa (\hat {\rho })\,\hat {\rho }\,a_{\min }\sin \vartheta . \end {aligned} \]

Adapting the multi-component reward structure of Pfau et al. \[ \begin {aligned} \mathcal {J}(\theta ) &= \frac {100}{T}\sum _{t=1}^{T} \operatorname {SmoothMax}_{\alpha = -3}\bigl (c_{\mathrm {LCFS},t},\, c_{I_p,t},\, c_{\mathrm {PF},t},\, c_{\mathrm {volt},t},\, c_{\mathrm {OH},t}\bigr ), \\[4pt] \operatorname {SmoothMax}_{\alpha }(x_1,\dots ,x_5) &= \frac {\sum _{m=1}^{5} x_m e^{\alpha x_m}}{\sum _{m=1}^{5} e^{\alpha x_m}}. \end {aligned} \]

6.3. Hardware Backends

Distance sweeps, top-\(K\) neighbor search, and candidate evaluation dispatch across three backends:

On Apple Silicon, custom Metal Shading Language (.metal) compute kernels run exact distance sweeps, top-\(K\) neighbor selection, FP32 device posteriors, and resident trust-region search (\(N \le 128\)) over unified memory, while general host f64 posterior and trajectory-draw calls execute on CPU.

On Linux x86_64 with NVIDIA GPUs (sm_75+), CUDA kernels evaluate pairwise distances via shared-memory (__shared__) tiles, global-memory top-\(K\) merge buffers, and Box–Muller Gaussian posterior draws.

Without a GPU, exact flat search runs f64 CPU scans alongside portable core::simd::f64x4 inner-product and squared-distance kernels compiled for the target architecture baseline (x86_64 / aarch64).

7. API Reference

7.1. State Transitions

OptimizerState is an immutable record exposing the incumbent .best_x, .best_y, decoded .best_params, observation arrays .x_obs and .y_obs (.num_observations), regional epoch counter .epoch, trust-region length .tr_length, and content-addressed .key derived from baux.digest(state).

baux.init(
    domain: np.ndarray | PyTreeSpec | Any,
    config: OptimizerConfig | Transform | None = None,
    *,
    pipe: OptimizerConfig | Transform | None = None,
    key: Key | None = None,
    seed: int | np.random.Generator | None = None,
    low: Any = -1.0,
    high: Any = 1.0,
) -> OptimizerState

baux.ask(state, num_arms=1) returns (next_state, batch), where AskBatch carries proposal IDs, coordinate arrays, decoded parameter dictionaries, and stacked tensors.

baux.tell(state, x, y, y_var=None) appends observations and returns (next_state, mu). baux.step(state, objective) runs one ask -> evaluate -> tell cycle.

baux.scan(state, key, steps, eval_fn, num_arms) folds transitions across rounds. baux.pmap(state, key, branches, branch_fn) forks snapshots and splits keys across worker threads.

Asynchronous regional completions and cancellations use baux.complete(state, ids, y, y_var=None) and baux.cancel(state, ids). States hash via baux.digest(state) (BLAKE3) and serialize via baux.freeze(state) and baux.thaw(state, data).

7.2. PRNG Keys

baux.key(seed) constructs a 128-bit (u64, u64) SplitMix64 key from an integer, byte buffer, array, state digest, or OS entropy, seeding deterministic ChaCha12 and optimizer streams. baux.split(k, n) partitions \(k\) into \(n\) subkeys, and baux.fold(k, tag) mixes in an integer tag without mutating \(k\), keeping proposal generation, hyperparameter fitting, and posterior sampling independent.

7.3. Pipeline Transforms

Pipelines compose Transform stages into an OptimizerConfig via baux.chain(*steps) or t1 | t2.

Initialization and perturbation use baux.lhd(num_init=10), baux.sobol(num_candidates=1024), baux.raasp(num_pert=20), and baux.uniform_pert(num_candidates=1024). Surrogates attach via baux.enn(k=10, geometry="euclidean"), baux.gp(), or baux.zero(); acquisitions attach via baux.ucb(beta=2.0), baux.thompson(), baux.pareto(), or baux.random_acq().

Geometry and regional layout are configured with baux.tr_bounds(length_init=0.8), baux.regions(count=4, sharing="ellipsoid_ball"), baux.with_geometry(target, "hyperbolic"), baux.stein(target, rank=4), and baux.calibrate(target, alpha=0.1).

7.4. PyTree Domains & Codomain Operators

Parameter spaces are written as nested Python dicts, tuples, and lists of leaf charts, compiled by baux.pytree_bounds(tree) into a PyTreeSpec.

Domain leaf charts include baux.Log(low, high), baux.Periodic(low, high), baux.Simplex(dim), and baux.categorical(categories, encoding="auto"). baux.ravel(tree, spec) and baux.unflatten(flat, spec) map between nested Python values and flat solver coordinates. Multi-output codomain operators use baux.Stiefel(loadings) (with baux.stein) and baux.Affine(scale, shift, conformal_q=1.0) (with baux.calibrate).

7.5. ENN Surrogate

baux.enn_init(train_x, train_y, scale_x=True) builds an immutable surrogate state; baux.append(state, x, y) appends observations via copy-on-write; baux.fit(state, k=10, key=k0) selects scale parameters by subsampled leave-one-out log-pseudolikelihood.

baux.posterior(state, x, params) evaluates posterior mean, aleatoric standard error, and epistemic standard error at x. baux.sample(state, x, params, key=k1, num_draws=16) returns (draws, idx), with function draws of shape (batch, metrics, num_draws) and neighbor indices of shape (batch, k).

7.6. Adapters

baux.create_optimizer(bounds=bounds, config=config, rng=rng) wraps the functional core in a stateful Optimizer exposing opt.ask(num_arms), opt.tell(x, y), opt.posterior_mu(x), and opt.telemetry(). from baux.optuna import Sampler provides an Optuna BaseSampler over continuous FloatDistribution spaces. baux.capabilities(probe=False) lists compiled and active backends ("cpu", "metal", "cuda", "opencl").
