Baux

baux is a black box optimization library built around Epistemic Nearest Neighbors (Bafna, Jadhav, & Sweet [BJS25]; yubo-research/enn) and multi-region local search. Rather than maintaining a global stationary covariance over evaluation history, baux forms local location–dispersion surrogates directly from precision-weighted \(K\)-nearest-neighbor neighborhoods, lifting points into non-stationary local-scale geometries that separate collapsed high-density basins from coarse exploratory regions.

Search domains decompose through section–retraction charts across product manifolds—multiplicative scales, circular phases, probability simplices, and constant-norm discrete codebooks—paired with Stiefel signal-subspace contraction on multi-output codomains. Observation history, epoch-stamped regional ledgers, and splittable 128-bit PRNG keys form an immutable state advanced by pure transitions (documented in the API Reference), supporting asynchronous out-of-order completions, deterministic parallel rollouts, and closed-loop policy search up to \(D \sim 1.6 \times 10^5\) parameters.

The loop below composes a hyperbolic-geometry ENN surrogate (baux.enn), upper-confidence acquisition (baux.ucb), and four trust regions with circumscribing-ball incumbent sharing (baux.regions) over a rotated, ill-conditioned 12D Rosenbrock ridge, advancing immutable state snapshots via baux.init, baux.ask, and baux.tell:

import numpy as np
import baux

dim = 12
axis = np.linspace(1.0, -1.0, dim)
rot = np.eye(dim) - 2.0 * np.outer(axis, axis) / float(axis @ axis)
scales = np.geomspace(1.0, 25.0, dim)


def valley_reward(x):
    z = (x @ rot.T) * (scales / 10.0)
    ridge = 20.0 * (z[:, 1:] - z[:, :-1] ** 2) ** 2 + (0.5 - z[:, :-1]) ** 2
    return -np.sum(ridge, axis=1)


bounds = np.tile([-2.0, 2.0], (dim, 1))
pipe = (
    baux.enn(k=10, geometry="hyperbolic")
    | baux.ucb(beta=2.0)
    | baux.regions(count=4, sharing="ellipsoid_ball")
)

state = baux.init(bounds, pipe, key=baux.key(42))
for _ in range(16):
    state, batch = baux.ask(state, num_arms=4)
    state, _ = baux.tell(state, batch, valley_reward(batch.points))

assert state.best_y > -25.0

1. Local Epistemic Geometry & Coherent Draws

1.1. Precision-Weighted Neighborhood Moments & Pseudolikelihood

Let \(\mathcal {X} \subseteq \mathbb {R}^D\) denote solver coordinates and \(\mathcal {D} = \{(x_m, y_m, s_m)\}_{m=1}^N \subset \mathcal {X} \times \mathbb {R} \times \mathbb {R}_+\) denote \(N\) heteroscedastic observations \(y_m = f(x_m) + s_m \varepsilon _m\) with \(\varepsilon _m \stackrel {\text {i.i.d.}}{\sim } \mathcal {N}(0, 1)\). Given a nonnegative pairwise dissimilarity \(\delta : \mathcal {X} \times \mathcal {X} \to \mathbb {R}_+\), empirical target variance scale \(\hat {\sigma }_y^2 > 0\), and dimensionless scale parameters \(\theta = (s_0^2, c_e) \in \mathbb {R}_+^2\), Epistemic Nearest Neighbors (Bafna et al. [BJS25]) assigns datum \(m\) at query \(x\) the working moments

\[ \mu (x \mid x_m) = y_m, \qquad \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_m) = \hat {\sigma }_y^2 s_0^2 + s_m^2, \qquad \sigma _e^2(x \mid x_m) = \hat {\sigma }_y^2 c_e\, \delta (x, x_m). \]

Writing \(N_K(x) \eqdef \{\pi _1(x), \dots , \pi _K(x)\}\) for the \(K\) nearest indices ordered by nondecreasing \(\delta (x, x_{\pi _i(x)})\) (ties broken by datum index) and precision weights \(w_i(x) \eqdef \bigl (\sigma _a^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)}) + \sigma _e^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)})\bigr )^{-1}\) on non-degenerate neighborhoods, the local predictive moments decompose epistemic and aleatoric dispersion in \(\mathcal {O}(N D + N \log K)\) exact scan time per query:

\[ \mu (x) = \frac {\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)\, y_{\pi _i(x)}}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \qquad \sigma _a^2(x) = \frac {\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)\, \sigma _a^2(x \mid x_{\pi _i(x)})}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \qquad \sigma _e^2(x) = \frac {1}{\sum _{i=1}^K w_i(x)}, \]

with total predictive variance \(\sigma ^2(x) \eqdef \sigma _e^2(x) + \sigma _a^2(x)\). Scale parameters \(\theta = (s_0^2, c_e)\) maximize a subsampled leave-one-out Gaussian log-pseudolikelihood over \(S_P \subset \{1, \dots , N\}\) of size \(P \le N\) [BJS25]:

\[ \widehat {\log \mathcal {L}}(\theta ) \eqdef -\frac {1}{2P} \sum _{n \in S_P} \left [\log \bigl (2\pi \sigma _{-n}^2(x_n; \theta )\bigr ) + \frac {\bigl (y_n - \mu _{-n}(x_n; \theta )\bigr )^2}{\sigma _{-n}^2(x_n; \theta )}\right ], \]

where \(\mu _{-n}\) and \(\sigma _{-n}^2\) exclude datum \(n\) from its own \(K\)-neighbor set.

1.2. Coherent Function Draws Across Order-\(K\) Voronoi Cells

Posterior sampling over candidate pools requires spatially correlated draws without factoring a \(Q \times Q\) joint query covariance. Tying standard normal perturbations to global datum indices induces positive spatial covariance wherever order-\(K\) Voronoi neighborhoods overlap:

Proposition 1.1 (Coherent Function Draws). Fix draw index \(s\) and output channel \(j\), and attach \(z_{s,m,j} \stackrel {\text {i.i.d.}}{\sim } \mathcal {N}(0, 1)\) to each datum \(m \in \{1, \dots , N\}\). Normalize the active precision weights and extend them by zero outside \(N_K(x)\) by setting \(\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x) \eqdef \bar {w}_{i,j}(x)\) if \(m = \pi _i(x) \in N_K(x)\) and \(0\) otherwise, where

\[ \bar {w}_{i,j}(x) \eqdef \frac {w_{i,j}(x)}{\sum _{r=1}^K w_{r,j}(x)}, \qquad \Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2 \eqdef \sqrt {\sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}^2(x)}, \qquad \tilde {f}_{s,j}(x) \eqdef \mu _j(x) + \frac {\sigma _{e,j}(x)}{\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2} \sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}(x)\, z_{s,\pi _i(x),j}. \]

Then for every \(x \in \mathcal {X}\), \(\tilde {f}_{s,j}(x) \sim \mathcal {N}\bigl (\mu _j(x),\, \sigma _{e,j}^2(x)\bigr )\), sample paths are smooth on the interior of each order-\(K\) Voronoi cell where \(N_K(x)\) is constant, and any \(x, x' \in \mathcal {X}\) with nonempty neighborhood intersection \(S_K(x, x') \eqdef N_K(x) \cap N_K(x') \ne \emptyset \) satisfy

\[ \operatorname {Cov}\bigl (\tilde {f}_{s,j}(x),\, \tilde {f}_{s,j}(x')\bigr ) = \frac {\sigma _{e,j}(x)\,\sigma _{e,j}(x')}{\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2\,\Vert \bar {w}_j(x')\Vert _2} \sum _{m \in S_K(x, x')} \tilde {w}_{m,j}(x)\,\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x') > 0. \]

Proof. Distinctness of \(\pi _1(x), \dots , \pi _K(x)\) implies that \(\xi _j(x) \eqdef \sum _{m=1}^N \tilde {w}_{m,j}(x)\, z_{s,m,j}\) is zero-mean Gaussian with variance \(\sum _{i=1}^K \bar {w}_{i,j}^2(x) = \Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2^2 > 0\); rescaling by \(\tfrac {\sigma _{e,j}(x)}{\Vert \bar {w}_j(x)\Vert _2}\) yields \(\tilde {f}_{s,j}(x) \sim \mathcal {N}(\mu _j(x), \sigma _{e,j}^2(x))\). For any \(x, x'\), \(\mathbb {E}[z_{s,m,j}\, z_{s,m',j}] = \delta _{m,m'}\) gives \(\operatorname {Cov}(\xi _j(x), \xi _j(x')) = \sum _{m \in S_K(x, x')} \tilde {w}_{m,j}(x)\,\tilde {w}_{m,j}(x')\), which is strictly positive whenever \(S_K(x, x') \ne \emptyset \) since every active precision weight is strictly positive.\(\;\square \)

1.3. Non-Stationary Local-Scale Dissimilarities

Sequential optimization concentrates evaluations exponentially inside active trust-region basins while leaving the global domain sparsely sampled. Let \(d(x, x') \eqdef \Vert x - x'\Vert _2\) denote Euclidean (or coordinate-scaled) distance and let \(\sigma (x) > 0\) denote the \(k_0\)-th positive Euclidean neighbor radius at \(x\) relative to \(\mathcal {D}\). Because \(\sigma (x)\) depends on the empirical configuration, rescales of \(d\) define symmetric nonnegative dissimilarities tailored to multi-scale sampling density:

Conformal normalization (Zelnik-Manor & Perona [ZP04]) rescales squared Euclidean separation by the geometric-mean endpoint scale:

\[ \delta _c(x, x_m) \eqdef \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)}. \]

Poincaré half-space geometry (Cannon et al. [CFK+97], Nickel & Kiela [NK17], Kisfaludi-Bak & van Wordragen [KvW24]) lifts each point into the upper half-space \(\mathbb {H}(\mathcal {X}) \eqdef \mathcal {X} \times \mathbb {R}_+\) equipped with \(ds^2 = \tfrac {\Vert dx\Vert _2^2 + d\sigma ^2}{\sigma ^2}\) via \(\Lambda (x) \eqdef (x, \sigma (x))\):

\[ \begin {CD} \mathcal {X} \times \mathcal {X} @>{\Lambda \times \Lambda }>> \mathbb {H}(\mathcal {X}) \times \mathbb {H}(\mathcal {X}) \\ @V{\delta _h}VV @VV{\cosh d_{\mathbb {H}} - 1}V \\ \mathbb {R}_+ @= \mathbb {R}_+ \end {CD} \]

Evaluating the isometric chordal invariant \(\cosh d_{\mathbb {H}}\bigl (\Lambda (x), \Lambda (x_m)\bigr ) - 1\) adds a dimensionless vertical scale-mismatch barrier to conformal horizontal distance:

\[ \delta _h(x, x_m) \eqdef \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{2\,\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)} + \frac {\bigl (\sigma (x) - \sigma (x_m)\bigr )^2}{2\,\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)}. \]

When a trust region collapses onto an incumbent basin (\(\sigma (x) \ll \sigma (x_m)\)), the vertical term scales as \(\mathcal {O}\Bigl (\tfrac {\sigma (x_m)}{\sigma (x)}\Bigr )\), quarantining local neighborhood estimates from coarse outer observations even at moderate Euclidean proximity, while symmetrically preventing exploratory queries in sparse regions from collapsing onto dense clusters. Replacing hyperbolic normalization with an additive 1D Bures–Wasserstein radius cost motivated by Gaussian optimal transport (Villani [Vil09], Peyré & Cuturi [PC19]) yields the hybrid transport dissimilarity:

\[ \delta _w(x, x_m) \eqdef \frac {d^2(x, x_m)}{\sqrt {\sigma (x)\,\sigma (x_m)}} + \bigl (\sigma (x) - \sigma (x_m)\bigr )^2. \]

The standalone functional interface (baux.enn_init, baux.with_geometry, baux.enn_fit, baux.enn_posterior, and baux.enn_sample) exposes these geometries directly. On a bimodal 6D design combining a tight low-noise basin with a coarse high-noise shell, Poincaré lifting isolates basin neighbors, separates epistemic (.se_epi) from heteroscedastic aleatoric (.se_ale) uncertainty, and produces spatially correlated draws across adjacent basin queries:

import numpy as np
import baux

rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
basin_x = 0.6 + 0.04 * rng.normal(size=(24, 6))
shell_x = rng.uniform(-1.5, 1.5, size=(24, 6))
train_x = np.vstack([basin_x, shell_x])

signal = np.exp(-1.5 * np.sum((train_x - 0.6) ** 2, axis=1, keepdims=True))
train_yvar = np.vstack([np.full((24, 1), 5e-4), np.full((24, 1), 5e-2)])
train_y = signal + np.sqrt(train_yvar) * rng.normal(size=(48, 1))

k_fit, k_draw = baux.split(baux.key(11), 2)
enn_state = baux.with_geometry(
    baux.enn_init(train_x, train_y, train_yvar, scale_x=True),
    "hyperbolic",
    local_k=4,
)
params = baux.enn_fit(enn_state, k=8, infer_aleatoric=False, key=k_fit)

queries = np.vstack([
    np.full((1, 6), 0.600),
    np.full((1, 6), 0.605),
    np.full((1, 6), 2.400),
])
post = baux.enn_posterior(
    enn_state, queries, params, flags=baux.enn.PosteriorFlags(observation_noise=True)
)
draws, idx = baux.enn_sample(enn_state, queries, params, key=k_draw, num_draws=64)

corr_basin = float(np.corrcoef(draws[0, 0], draws[1, 0])[0, 1])
assert np.all(idx[0] < 24)
assert post.se_epi[0, 0] < post.se_epi[2, 0]
assert np.allclose(post.se**2, post.se_epi**2 + post.se_ale**2)
assert corr_basin > 0.9

2. Product Manifolds & Subspace Contraction

2.1. Section–Retraction Charts on Product Spaces

Structured parameter spaces factor as products \(\mathcal {M} = \prod _{\ell =1}^L \mathcal {M}_\ell \) across multiplicative scales, periodic phases, probability simplices, and discrete sets. Each factor \(\mathcal {M}_\ell \) is equipped with a section \(\psi _\ell : \mathcal {M}_\ell \to \mathcal {U}_\ell \subseteq \mathbb {R}^{d_\ell }\) into flat solver coordinates and a retraction \(\rho _\ell : \mathcal {U}_\ell \to \mathcal {M}_\ell \) satisfying \(\rho _\ell \circ \psi _\ell = \mathrm {id}_{\mathcal {M}_\ell }\), so neighbor search and trust-region perturbations operate in \(\mathcal {U} = \prod _\ell \mathcal {U}_\ell \) while evaluations retract cleanly onto \(\mathcal {M}\):

\[ \begin {CD} \mathcal {M}_\ell @>{\psi _\ell }>> \mathcal {U}_\ell \\ @V{\mathrm {id}_{\mathcal {M}_\ell }}VV @VV{\rho _\ell }V \\ \mathcal {M}_\ell @= \mathcal {M}_\ell \end {CD} \]

Euclidean distance on \(\mathcal {U}_\ell \) pulls back to intrinsic geometry on each factor:

  • Multiplicative intervals (baux.Log): On \([a, b] \subset \mathbb {R}_+\), the logarithmic section and exponential retraction \[ \psi (x) = \frac {\log x - \log a}{\log b - \log a} \in [0, 1], \qquad \rho (u) = a\left (\frac {b}{a}\right )^{\Pi _{[0,1]}(u)} \] pull back Euclidean distance to the scale-invariant metric \(ds^2 = \tfrac {dx^2}{x^2}\) up to \(\bigl (\log b - \log a\bigr )^{-2}\).
  • Circular phases (baux.Periodic): On \(\lbrack a, b\rparen \cong S^1\) of period \(P = b - a\) with phase \(\phi (x) = \tfrac {2\pi (x - a)}{P}\), the unit-circumference embedding and \(\operatorname {atan2}\) retraction \[ \psi (x) = \left (\frac {1}{2} + \frac {\cos \phi (x)}{2\pi },\; \frac {1}{2} + \frac {\sin \phi (x)}{2\pi }\right ), \qquad d_{\mathcal {U}}\bigl (\psi (x), \psi (x')\bigr ) = \frac {1}{\pi }\left |\sin \left (\frac {\phi (x) - \phi (x')}{2}\right )\right | \] induce chordal distance on \(S^1\) without boundary-wrap discontinuities.
  • Probability simplices (baux.Simplex): On \(\Delta ^{d-1}\), the positive-orthant square-root section and projective retraction \[ \psi (p) = \bigl (\sqrt {p_1}, \dots , \sqrt {p_d}\bigr ) \in S_+^{d-1}, \qquad \rho (u) = \frac {\max (u, 0)^{\odot 2}}{\Vert \max (u, 0)\Vert _2^2} \] pull back the ambient metric to \(\tfrac {1}{4}\,g_{\mathrm {FR}}\) on \(\operatorname {int}(\Delta ^{d-1})\), with squared chordal distance matching squared Hellinger distance \(\sum _{j=1}^d \bigl (\sqrt {p_j} - \sqrt {q_j}\bigr )^2\).
  • Constant-norm discrete codebooks (baux.categorical): Finite sets \(\{1, \dots , C\}\) embed into \(\mathbb {R}^{d_c}\) via constant-norm orthogonal or Rademacher codewords \(\psi (c)\), reducing nearest-codeword retraction \(\rho (u) = \arg \min _c \Vert u - \psi (c)\Vert _2\) to maximum inner-product decoding \(\arg \max _c \langle u, \psi (c)\rangle \).

2.2. Stiefel Signal-Subspace Contraction

When evaluations return correlated multi-output vectors \(y = \theta + \eta \in \mathbb {R}^M\) concentrated near a low-rank signal subspace, a loading matrix \(L \in \mathbb {R}^{M \times r}\) (\(1 \le r < M\)) orthonormalizes onto the Stiefel manifold \(V = L(L^\top L)^{-\frac {1}{2}} \in \mathrm {St}(r, M)\) (baux.Stiefel) with orthogonal complement \(V_\perp \in \mathrm {St}(M-r, M)\). Under \(P \eqdef (V^\top ,\, V_\perp ^\top )^\top : \mathbb {R}^M \xrightarrow {\cong } \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r}\), subspace contraction leaves tangent coordinates \(z_\parallel = V^\top y\) intact while shrinking normal coordinates \(z_\perp = V_\perp ^\top y\) (Stein [Ste56], James & Stein [JS61]):

\[ \begin {CD} \mathbb {R}^M @>{P}>> \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r} \\ @V{\hat {T}_{\alpha }}VV @VV{I_r \oplus \alpha I_{M-r}}V \\ \mathbb {R}^M @<<{P^\top }< \mathbb {R}^r \oplus \mathbb {R}^{M-r} \end {CD} \]

With zero-mean noise variances \(v_\parallel \eqdef \mathbb {E}\Vert V^\top \eta \Vert _2^2\) and \(v_\perp \eqdef \mathbb {E}\Vert V_\perp ^\top \eta \Vert _2^2 > 0\) and off-subspace signal energy \(b_\perp ^2 \eqdef \Vert V_\perp ^\top \theta \Vert _2^2\), the linear class \(\hat {\theta }_\alpha = V z_\parallel + \alpha V_\perp z_\perp \) attains its minimum quadratic risk \(\mathcal {R}(\alpha ) = \mathbb {E}\Vert \hat {\theta }_\alpha - \theta \Vert _2^2\) at the oracle contraction factor \(\alpha ^*\), strictly improving upon unshrunk estimation:

\[ \alpha ^* = \frac {b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp }, \qquad \mathcal {R}(\alpha ^*) = v_\parallel + \frac {v_\perp b_\perp ^2}{b_\perp ^2 + v_\perp } < v_\parallel + v_\perp = \mathcal {R}(1). \]

Under isotropic Gaussian noise in normal codimension \(M - r \ge 3\), positive-part James–Stein shrinkage uniformly dominates \(\alpha = 1\) ([JS61], Efron & Morris [EM73]), whereas baux.stein applies the empirical moment-ratio plug-in across any \(M - r \ge 1\):

\[ \hat {\alpha } = \frac {\Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2}{\Vert z_\perp \Vert _2^2 + \hat {v}_\perp }. \]

When held-out calibration pairs are available, baux.calibrate rescales predictive dispersion via affine weighted least squares and standardized residual quantiles, yielding finite-sample coverage on exchangeable splits and an empirical dispersion adjustment on adaptive optimization histories where sequential selection breaks exchangeability.

Below, a PyTree product domain combining logarithmic gain, periodic phase, simplex filter weights, and categorical windowing drives a noisy 6-channel spectral response concentrated near a rank-2 Stiefel frame:

import math
import numpy as np
import baux

rng = np.random.default_rng(3)
space = {
    "gain": baux.Log(1e-3, 1.0),
    "phase": baux.Periodic(0.0, 2.0 * math.pi),
    "weights": baux.Simplex(4),
    "window": baux.categorical(["hann", "hamming", "blackman", "kaiser"]),
}
loadings = np.array([
    [1.0, 0.0],
    [0.8, 0.2],
    [0.2, 0.9],
    [-0.4, 0.7],
    [0.5, -0.5],
    [-0.3, -0.6],
])
frame = baux.Stiefel(loadings)
target_w = np.array([0.45, 0.30, 0.15, 0.10])
win_loss = {"hann": 0.0, "hamming": 0.04, "blackman": 0.08, "kaiser": 0.01}


def spectral_channels(p):
    g_err = math.log10(p["gain"] / 0.05)
    ph_err = math.sin(0.5 * (p["phase"] - 1.2))
    w_err = float(np.sum((np.sqrt(p["weights"]) - np.sqrt(target_w)) ** 2))
    latent = np.array([-(g_err**2 + w_err + win_loss[p["window"]]), -(ph_err**2)])
    return loadings @ latent + 0.15 * rng.normal(size=6)


pipe = (
    baux.enn(k=8, geometry="conformal")
    | baux.stein(frame)
    | baux.ucb(beta=1.5)
    | baux.regions(count=3)
)
state = baux.init(space, pipe, key=baux.key(19))
for _ in range(10):
    state, batch = baux.ask(state, num_arms=3)
    y = np.vstack([spectral_channels(p) for p in batch.params])
    state, mu_shrunk = baux.tell(state, batch, y)

assert mu_shrunk.shape == (3, 6) and state.y_obs.shape == (30, 6)

3. Functional State & Asynchronous Portfolios

3.1. Pure State Transitions & Epoch-Stamped Regional Ledgers

Optimization state is an immutable tuple in the product space

\[ S \eqdef \mathcal {K} \times \mathcal {D} \times \prod _{r=1}^R T_r \times \mathrm {Spec}(\mathcal {M}), \]

joining a splittable 128-bit PRNG key \(k \in \mathcal {K}\) (Claessen & Pałka [CP13], Steele, Lea, & Flood [SLF14]), global observation history \(\mathcal {D}\), \(R\) concurrent local trust-region ledgers \(\{T_r(x_{0,r}, \ell _r, g_r)\}_{r=1}^R\) (Eriksson et al. [EPG+19], Daulton et al. [DEB+22]), and the compiled domain chart specification \(\mathrm {Spec}(\mathcal {M})\). Multi-objective portfolios (\(M \ge 2\)) draw per-region simplex weights to scalarize normalized objectives [DEB+22], while arm allocation across regions balances normalized incumbent value against completed plus in-flight evaluations.

Asynchronous evaluation decouples global surrogate updates from regional side-length adaptation via epoch-stamped tickets \(\mathrm {AskId} = (r, g, a)\), recording originating region \(r\), its generation epoch \(g = g_r\) at dispatch, and arm slot \(a\). Out-of-order completions via baux.complete always append observations to \(\mathcal {D}\), whereas trust-region expansion/contraction on \(T_r\) applies if and only if \(g = g_r\)—preventing delayed evaluations from a pre-restart epoch from perturbing a freshly re-centered basin—and baux.cancel releases in-flight slot reservations without altering \(\mathcal {D}\). Cross-region coordination is governed by incumbent adoption: sharing="origin_only" restricts \(x_{0,r}\) updates to region-\(r\) proposals, whereas sharing="ellipsoid_ball" adopts any superior global observation falling inside the Euclidean ball circumscribing \(T_r\)'s solver-coordinate box around \(x_{0,r}\), propagating local gains across overlapping basins while preserving distinct modes.

3.2. Splittable Rollouts & Canonical Snapshot Commutativity

Because appending observations alters neighbor sets and regional incumbents, baux.ask and baux.tell form an order-dependent Mealy transition system rather than commuting updates. Reproducibility instead rests on pure functional folding (baux.scan), pre-split parallel branch keys \(k \mapsto (k_1, \dots , k_B)\) (baux.pmap) that render parallel rollouts invariant to worker scheduling, and lossless binary checkpointing. For a template state \(s_0 \in S\) fixing \(\mathrm {Spec}(\mathcal {M})\) and any pure step transition \(\Phi _f : S \to S\), canonical serialization baux.freeze \(: S \to \mathcal {B}\) and deserialization baux.thaw \(: \mathcal {B} \to S\) commute with rollout execution \(\widehat {\Phi }_f \eqdef \mathrm {freeze} \circ \Phi _f \circ \mathrm {thaw}_{s_0}\):

\[ \begin {CD} S @>{\Phi _f}>> S \\ @V{\mathrm {freeze}}VV @AA{\mathrm {thaw}_{s_0}}A \\ \mathcal {B} @>>{\widehat {\Phi }_f}> \mathcal {B} \end {CD} \]

The example below dispatches overlapping regional batches, completes and cancels tickets out of order via batch.ids, verifies canonical baux.freeze/baux.thaw idempotence and baux.digest parity, and confirms bit-identical parallel baux.pmap rollout digests across restored snapshots:

import numpy as np
import baux

bounds = np.tile([-3.0, 3.0], (8, 1))
weights = np.geomspace(1.0, 16.0, 8)


def anisotropic_loss(x):
    return -np.sum(weights * (x - 0.35) ** 2 + 0.1 * np.cos(3.0 * x), axis=1)


pipe = (
    baux.lhd(8)
    | baux.enn(k=6)
    | baux.thompson()
    | baux.regions(count=4, sharing="ellipsoid_ball")
)
s0 = baux.init(bounds, pipe, key=baux.key(101))
s_warm, b_init = baux.ask(s0, num_arms=8)
s_warm = baux.complete(s_warm, b_init.ids, anisotropic_loss(b_init.points))

s_live, b1 = baux.ask(s_warm, num_arms=2)
s_live, b2 = baux.ask(s_live, num_arms=2)
s_live = baux.complete(s_live, [b2.ids[0]], anisotropic_loss(b2.points[:1]))
s_live = baux.cancel(s_live, [b2.ids[1]])
s_live = baux.complete(s_live, b1.ids, anisotropic_loss(b1.points))

blob = baux.freeze(s_live)
restored_a = baux.thaw(s0, blob)
restored_b = baux.thaw(s0, baux.freeze(restored_a))
assert baux.freeze(restored_a) == blob
assert baux.digest(restored_a) == baux.digest(restored_b)

rollouts_a = baux.pmap(
    restored_a,
    baux.key(77),
    branches=4,
    branch_fn=lambda i, st, k: baux.digest(
        baux.scan(st, k, steps=4, eval_fn=anisotropic_loss, num_arms=2)[0]
    ),
)
rollouts_b = baux.pmap(
    restored_b,
    baux.key(77),
    branches=4,
    branch_fn=lambda i, st, k: baux.digest(
        baux.scan(st, k, steps=4, eval_fn=anisotropic_loss, num_arms=2)[0]
    ),
)
assert rollouts_a == rollouts_b and len(set(rollouts_a)) == 4

4. Closed-Loop Tokamak Control at \(D \sim 1.5 \times 10^5\)

Motivated by closed-loop tokamak magnetic and profile control (Degrave et al. [Deg+22], Tracey et al. [Tra+24], Pfau et al. [Pfa+25]), we study direct parameter-space policy search across two contrasting evaluation regimes. In the sample-scarce regime on the EPFL/MIT toka_gym TCV rampdown benchmark (Wang et al. [Wan+25], Theiler et al. [The+26]), a four-layer neural policy (\(D = 136{,}712\) weights) is optimized over \(100\) sequential rounds (\(n = 10\) paired seeds): multi-region ENN-TuRBO (\(K = 10\), \(R = 10\)) reduces tracking error from 65.72 at initialization to 1.68 (origin-only) and 1.79 (ellipsoid-ball), compared to 20.59 for on-policy PPO under the same interaction budget.

To isolate cross-region incumbent sharing over long horizons free of surrogate fitting variance, we evaluate a \(93 \to 256 \to 256 \to 256 \to 19\) policy (\(D = 160{,}531\) weights) over \(10{,}000\) rounds in TORAX 2.0.0 (Citrin et al. [Cit+24]), coupling four 1D radial core-transport PDEs with Miller local-equilibrium shaping (Miller et al. [Mil+98]). Across \(n = 10\) paired seeds, circumscribing-ball adoption (ellipsoid-ball) reaches 91.57 ± 0.55 versus 89.41 ± 0.97 for independent regions (origin-only; paired difference +2.16 ± 0.91, unadjusted \(t_9\) \(p_2 = 0.0410\), Holm–Bonferroni \(p_{\mathrm {Holm}} = 0.246\) across six checkpoints; PPO reaches 44.25). Full transport/reward formulations, checkpoint curves, and an interactive multi-trust-region simulator spanning all seven BAUX benchmark families (host-regions, resident-regions, tokamak, oceananigans, fuse, hardcaml, and pretrain) are available in the Playground.

5. Zero-Copy Shared-Memory Physics & Seven-Benchmark Suite

External scientific PDE and integrated plant solvers connect to the Rust/Metal optimizer without a C-ABI shim or per-step serialization. Candidate batches, scalarized or Pareto objective vectors, constraint violations, and diagnostic state fields share a 16 KB page-aligned mmap slab (ShmSlab) coordinated by 128-byte cache-line-isolated Release/Acquire atomic u64 doorbells in page 0. On Apple Silicon, unit-cube proposals stored as IEEE FP16 in unified GPU/CPU memory decode and affine-scale directly into mapped f32 candidate slots via NEON intrinsics with zero heap allocation, while persistent Unix-domain-socket Julia daemons retain JIT-warmed per-thread solver pools across runs:

  • 3D Geophysical Fluid Dynamics (Oceananigans.jl 0.117.2): Nonhydrostatic Kelvin–Helmholtz shear-instability suppression (\(64 \times 1 \times 64 \times 2\) grid, \(D = 8{,}192\) boundary/forcing weights) and wind-driven mixed-layer deepening (Ramadhan et al. [Ram+20]) evaluate across Julia worker threads over ShmSlab in 0.24s–0.47s worker time (0.34s–0.88s end-to-end warm attach).
  • Integrated Fusion Plant & Active Coil Synthesis (FUSE.jl 1.2.1): Whole-facility synthesis (ActorWholeFacility), coupled core-transport and plasma-current evolution (ActorDynamicPlasma), and free-boundary active poloidal-field shape control (ActorEquilibrium(:TEQUILA) + ActorPFdesign(:uniform) + ActorPFactive + ActorPlasmaLimits; Meneghini et al. [Men+24]) evaluate against persistent warm daemons on SPARC, reducing multi-round turnaround from 207.7s cold JIT startup to 5.83s worker time (8.68s total).
  • Native Rust/Metal Evaluators & Browser Playground: Pure-Rust/Metal tokamak transport rollouts (0.11s per seed across 10 seeds), resident Apple Metal GPU multi-region loops (resident-regions), host multi-trust-region landscapes (host-regions), multi-objective HardCaml RTL LUT/DSP/cycle Pareto synthesis (hardcaml), and autoregressive token-mixture/schedule search (pretrain) unify with oceananigans and fuse in the Playground—compiled from typed OCaml via js_of_ocaml and verified by ./baux pages build | check | serve under sha384 Subresource Integrity and a default-src 'none' Content Security Policy.
